## 1. House writing rules

Full rules: `rules/writing-rules.md`. In short: no em dash and no en dash, ever. Use commas or restructure. Banned words: leverage, unlock, seamless, robust, streamline, cutting-edge, empower, and their variants. Short paragraphs. Specific numbers and named mechanisms over adjectives. Commonwealth spelling as the sources use it: programme, organisation, colour, behaviour. Every figure carries its source. Describe what the service does, never what it has achieved.

The register is a serious document read by an accountable marketing leader deciding where a large budget goes. It reads like an argument with citations, not a brochure. Honesty about being new is a selling asset here, not a confession.

## 5. What The Warm Channel is

**A done-for-you B2B lead generation channel built on in-person events.** Strategy, execution, the people in the room, and the work afterwards that turns them into sales-qualified leads. The client runs their business; we run the channel.

A client names the companies it wants as customers. The Warm Channel plans the channel, gets the right people from those companies into the room, and works the conversations afterwards until the client's sellers have sales-qualified leads. The approved short line is "This isn't an events agency. This is a lead generation channel that happens to run in person."

**In person spans many formats**: an executive dinner, a roundtable, a hosted breakfast, a summit, a side event at a conference, a suite at a trade show, a private session inside an exhibition. A vendor planning a 200 person summit and one planning a table of ten are both customers. **The format is a delivery detail and never appears in a client headline.**

**What it is not.** Not attendance, which is one step of four and undersells the whole. Not convening, which is a component. Not an events agency, which is paid to produce an event. The Warm Channel supplies no venue, no catering and no production. The client hosts and contracts those directly. This was a founder constraint for cost reasons, removing venue liability, insurance exposure and catering float, and it turned out to be the position.

**The dream outcome the page sells**: highly qualified B2B sales leads at the moment every other channel is degrading. Not leads, MQLs or meetings. Sales-qualified leads from people who met a human being, in a room, for long enough to form a view.

**The tailwind**: cold email reply rates fell from 8.5% in 2019 to 3.43% in 2026 (Instantly, grade B) while AI sending tools pushed four to seven times the volume into the same inboxes (grade C plus). Channels that can be automated are being flooded. The one where a person has to physically turn up cannot be.

**The name** was built for this positioning: a lead generation channel whose output is warm. It is not a claim that a warm network already exists.

**The business today.** Solo founder, pre-launch, in Toronto. No entity, no business name registered, no tax number, no website, no employees or contractors. Burn and runway `{TBD}`. Adjacent history: founder-led LinkedIn and email outreach filled two commissioned rooms in another business, with a self-reported reply rate that is unaudited and banned until D3.

## 6. Offer components, what leads and what is table stakes

The channel is sold as one bundled engagement. These components are configured on the sales call and **never become website sections**, because a buyer offered four components buys the cheapest and judges the channel on it. From Run A Phase 1, six vectors, rebuilt 2026-09-22.

| Component | Demand | Differentiation | Effort (1 to 10) | Funnel role | Quadrant |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Post-event conversion | 17.6 | 8.3 | 6, runs for weeks | Where the outcome is produced | Hero |
| Audience build | 18.9 | 1.6 | 9, the founder carries it | The revenue step | Hero, barely differentiated |
| Measurement and attribution | 16.1 | 7.4 | 3, once instrumented | Retention, funds the second engagement | Hero, the sleeper |
| Channel strategy | 12.4 | 9.8 | 4 | Entry into the bundle | Hero |

| Programme running | 11.2 | minus 13.5 | 7, non-scaling | Quality control | Table stakes, never priced separately |

**What leads messaging**: post-event conversion, because it is the step the category is documented as skipping and the only component that produces what the page promises. It is also BLOCKED by D11. Settle D11 and the hero component is usable.

**The demand driver**: the Channel Coverage Read. The client sends its named target account list and gets back how many accounts have a reachable decision maker at the target title, which events those people already attend, and what a realistic composed audience from that list looks like. It separates a coverage problem from an access problem, which feel identical from inside. Variants: full read (white glove, priority accounts), one-segment sample (the workhorse, offered on the first call), channel economics calculator (built), event portfolio teardown (public, content), coverage scorecard (self-assessment, IN BUILD). ## 7. Competitive market analysis

Sources: `research/` and Run A Phase 2 and 3. Method was search only. No competitor page was read directly and **buyer-side complaint data does not exist**, so every weakness is structural, not reported. Full detail: `reference/competitive-analysis.md` and `reference/competitor-battlecards.md`.

**The comparison set changed with the product.** Against a lead generation channel the buyer compares anyone a marketing leader would fund instead:

1. **Executive event agencies** (ConvergeX Connections, The Ortus Club, MyOutreach, Clutch, CXO & Co). Closest substitute. Paid to produce an event and fill it. Pay-on-attendance is a category norm among the better ones, not our differentiator. ConvergeX is reported to guarantee 50% of attendees book a post-event meeting (grade C plus, unconfirmed).
2. **Outbound, demand generation and SDR agencies.** Compete on volume in channels automation is degrading.
3. **The in-house route**: the field marketing function on owned software (Splash, Vendelux, Goldcast, Attendir), or a new hire. Toronto field marketing manager average $94,728 base (ERI SalaryExpert, grade B). Employer cost above base `{TBD}`.
4. **The status quo**: trade show booths and badge scans. The real competitor, because the budget renews itself.
5. **Hosted-buyer and summit operators** (Gartner Evanta, Argyle, GDS Group, Millennium Alliance). Sell access to an audience they own. The sponsor does not choose who is in the room.

**The axis the category runs on**: does the operator sell access to an audience it owns, or composition of the client's own named list? The Warm Channel is the second, by design.

**Positioning vectors.** Delivered Certainty and Event Craft are led by competitors, and we score near zero honestly. Two structural vectors are ours: **Access Without Handover**, where the client reaches people it cannot reach and keeps the host position, the venue and the relationship, and **Promise Discipline**, which earns its score only once D4 sets a promise and a remedy. There is no vector on which we beat ConvergeX at what ConvergeX is for. A buyer who wants a meeting count should buy ConvergeX. Select the buyer, do not argue with them.

**Three things slot 1 does not appear to do** (hypotheses from a search pass, never "only we"): sell the coverage read standalone, publish a composition report, or focus on our chosen segment. Each needs a direct site read before it is claimed.

## 8. Ideal customer profile

### Client, Level 1, the five qualifying characteristics

Defined by characteristics, not geography. **The page never names a country or a city.** All five must hold, in priority order:

1. **High average contract value**, six figures and up. Below it the arithmetic of a composed audience does not work. Check: ask for ACV.
2. **Already spends materially on events.** We sell a better return from a channel they fund. Check: which events did they exhibit at or sponsor last year.
3. **The buyer is structurally unreachable cold.** If a sequence reaches them, the cheapest channel wins. Check: their current outbound reply rate to that title.
4. **They already distinguish warm from cold.** No education required. Check: what a referred opportunity closes at against a cold one.
5. **Current event spend converts badly.** The cost of the problem. Check: what last year's flagship produced in pipeline. Not knowing is the answer.

**The wedge**: trade show exhibiting is 40.8% of exhibitor marketing budgets, the largest single channel line (CEIR 2026, grade B), and mid-market reports roughly 1% in-person conversion from trade show leads (grade C). The money is already being spent, and it is being spent badly. Same budget, redirected.

### Level 2, the concentration segment, OPEN (D8)

| Segment | Case | Against |
|---|---|---|
| **Enterprise cybersecurity vendors**, working default | Strongest unreachability (CISOs reportedly field around 60 cold approaches a week, grade C), RSA and Black Hat spend, security buys on peer reference | The 2026-09-16 brief rejected it as saturated, with CISOs avoiding vendor dinners. 68% of CISOs say aggressive marketing hides real innovation (grade C plus) |
| Healthcare and life sciences technology into health systems | The only segment where the founder has operating history | The founder was leaving healthcare to a separate business. The healthcare guest list is never ported, D6 |
| Fintech and financial infrastructure into banks and insurers | The original brief's choice. High ACV, event-heavy | A bank VP is more reachable than a CISO. That fintech is uncrowded was never tested |

Most segment evidence is grade C. If the first ten security conversations go badly on credibility rather than fit, switch. The switch costs only the vertical wording.

**Disqualify**: ACV under six figures; no named account list; wants a booth, venue or crew; needs a guaranteed meeting or lead count to report upward; does not believe in events. **Refuse or reprice** the tempting one: a buyer who wants one event, run once, to see if it works, because the channel's output arrives weeks later and a test gets judged on the day.

**Problem maturity**: a vendor that has run an event and been disappointed is the better buyer, because it knows what the failure costs. A vendor that never has needs two sales, so it enters through the coverage read.

### Buying committee

| | User | Manager | Decision maker |
|---|---|---|---|
| Titles | Field marketing, demand generation, events or ABM manager | VP Marketing, Head of Demand Generation | CMO, CRO where budget crosses to sales |
| Role | Recommends. **Can kill it silently** | Approves at typical size. The real decision | Signs at programme size |
| Blocks on | "This is a judgment on my competence" | "This reads as my team failing", and "what do I report upward" | Cannot tie it to pipeline. Procurement rejects an unregistered supplier, D1 |

**Sell capacity, never competence.** The failure is arithmetic, not skill: getting named senior people to commit takes weeks of individual outreach that no generalist has free. Nobody underperformed. The arithmetic was never done.

### Guest ICP

The guest is user, manager and decision maker in one person, deciding alone in under a minute on a phone. **Anchor guests** (VP and above at institutions the others recognise) are secured first, and **accessible guests** (Director level) are built around them, because acceptance runs downhill and not uphill. The founding guest cohort target is twenty executives across at least three functions: four to six anchors and fourteen to sixteen accessible guests. The original guest ICP was Canadian bank, credit union and insurer executives, which followed the old client ICP. It must be re-derived once D8 picks the segment (D14). Guest inventory starts at zero.

## 13. Customer journeys

Full stage tables: `reference/customer-journey-client.md` and `reference/guest-journey.md`.

**Client, seven stages.** 1 problem awareness: the last event produced badge scans, not buyers. 2 solution discovery: somebody must run this as a channel. 3 evaluation: is this person real, what exactly am I buying. **The coverage read is the hinge**: value before payment, no track record needed, a number about their own list. 4 purchase: explain it upward without it sounding like failure, and an invoice procurement accepts. 5 value realisation: are the right people confirming. 6 expansion: the first reference, which ends the empty proof set. 7 renewal: a programme.

**Accelerators**: lead with the coverage read. Run the demonstration room. Reconstruct the two commissioned rooms (D3). Settle the promise (D4).

**Guest, seven stages.** 1 the question registers as real. 2 who is this and how did they get my name, where most approaches die silently. 3 who else is going, where a thin room is disclosed honestly. 4 acceptance. 5 reconfirmation and arrival. 6 the evening, held to format. 7 **second acceptance**, the only stage that compounds.

**Capacity**: one person runs every row. Two concurrent engagements is probably the ceiling. That is a pricing input and a hiring trigger.

## 14. Channel and asset plan

Full method: `reference/channel-engine.md`. Three client-side channels, three guest-side routes.

**Client side**
1. **Founder-led organic and low-volume, high-research direct approach.** The acquisition motion demonstrates the product. Assets: public event composition teardown (the demand driver in public), the category post (vendor-owned conferences attend at 19%, invite-only executive dinners at 70%, Clutch Events, 13,155 registrations across 191 events, grade B plus), the coverage read, the ROI calculator, the composition report template, the scope document (BLOCKED).
2. **The demonstration room**, at the founder's own cost, prospective clients invited to observe and disclosed to guests. It produces the first reference, the first guest cohort and the first auditable funnel table at once. BLOCKED by D5 and runway.
3. **Partnerships** with associations and service providers already selling to the segment. Borrowed credibility and a second route to guest supply. The 2026-09-18 partner set (CBA, CCUA, Advocis, Payments Canada) served the fintech segment, so re-source it after D8 (D15).

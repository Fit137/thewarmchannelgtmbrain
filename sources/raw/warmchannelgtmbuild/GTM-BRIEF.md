# The Warm Channel, GTM Builder Discovery Brief

The filled discovery input for the `gtm-builder` skill. This is what the skill's REQUIRED INPUTS checklist asks for, answered.

Read `SKILL-ADAPTATION.md` before Phase 1. The skill is written for B2B SaaS and this is a done-for-you service sold as an engagement, so six of the nine phases need a documented substitution, and the run happens twice because there are two audiences.

**Global rule for the whole run,** taken from the skill's own deck prompt and applied everywhere: if a metric is unknown, keep it as `{TBD}` and do not invent numbers. The Warm Channel is pre-launch, with no entity, no website, no client, no revenue and no delivered engagement, so the proof set is empty and closed and every proof point and benchmark is `{TBD}` until supplied. The founder's prior record is real and is a credential rather than a proof point, and Section 5 sets out what may be said about it and in what wording.

**Source:** founder briefing, spoken, 2026-09-16, plus a market research pass and strategy conclusion produced in the same session and dated 2026-09-16. Prior operating history read from the `Fit137/medtechnorthcontext` repository, `HANDOFF.md` and `docs/12-convening-as-a-service.md`, as of 2026-09-18.

## Classification

The four facts everything downstream reads from.

| | |
|---|---|
| Archetype | Agency, reading as services throughout. The competitive set includes in-housing, which is not a company |
| Audiences | Two. Run A, the paying client, a fintech or financial infrastructure vendor. Run B, the non-paying guest, a bank, credit union or insurer executive |
| Evidence state | Pre-launch. Proof set empty and closed |
| Motion | Sales-led and founder-led for Run A. Nomination and direct approach for Run B. Not self-serve, not paid |

## Run order

`gtm-builder` builds one GTM per run, so this runs twice. The two audiences have different buyers, different offers and different value propositions, and only one of them pays. Blending them produces Phase 6 pillars that are true of neither and a Phase 8 channel set that serves one and wastes the other.

**Run A, the client, goes first.** Its offer is closest to a product, its competitor research is tractable, and the pricing phase has something to price. Run B can borrow structure from Phases 3, 6 and 7 of Run A, but it must not borrow the copy, for the reason set out in Section 5.

**The dependency that is easy to miss.** Run B is the supply side. The business cannot deliver Run A without it, and guest-side inventory, not the method, is the asset that compounds. Run B being second in sequence does not make it second in importance.

**Nothing below is confirmed as a competitor.** The named companies were surfaced in a search pass on 2026-09-16 and have not been crawled, priced or checked for relevance to the fintech niche. Research from job postings and read the compensation band, required experience and time to hire as its cost and its weakness | The build-it or hire-it alternative. **Selection rule:** a competitor earns a slot only if the buyer actually evaluates it. Not who the founder thinks of as a rival, who the buyer considers instead. For this business the most likely honest answer in slot 2 is that the buyer does nothing, or asks an existing employee to try, and slot 4 is what they will name out loud while slot 2 is what they will actually do.

# 5. POSITIONING AND CLAIM CONSTRAINTS

Phase 3 writes positioning statements, Phase 6 writes twenty-seven headlines across nine copy drills, and the deck compresses all of it. All three will reach for the claims below, so the narrowed form has to be here rather than remembered.

**The governing sensitivity, and it is sharper here than in most businesses.** The two audiences occupy the same room, and one of them is being sold to the other. Client-facing copy describes composed attendance and is read by people buying access. Guest-facing copy describes a room of peers and a live question. A guest who reads the client-facing language learns they were inventory, and the guest list is the only asset this business has. Keep two vocabularies and never let the client vocabulary reach a guest-facing surface. The trust-scarcity argument in particular is a client argument, and running it at guests tells them their own professional presence is noise.

## The narrowed claims

| Do not write | Write instead |
|---|---|
| I raised a $30 million Series A from investors after these trade shows and exhibitions | Served as CMO at Aimedis during a period in which the company raised a $30M Series A, and led the exhibition and trade show programme that preceded it. Counter-example: a Series A is raised by a company through a process with many inputs, and no investor would confirm the exhibitions caused it. Gate on documentation before any external use |
| Resulted in more than $1 million in pipeline deals from hospital and pharma buyers | `{TBD}` until the artifact exists. Then: ran the exhibition and trade show programme at Aimedis, to which the company's own reporting attributed pipeline in excess of $1M in qualified opportunities from hospital and pharma buyers. Counter-example: pipeline is not revenue, the figure is unverifiable without a CRM export, and resulted in is sole causation |
| A roughly 70% positive reply rate | `{TBD}`. Do not write it anywhere until reconstructed from send records. Counter-example: positive reply is undefined, a polite refusal is a reply, the denominator was small and deliberately capped by venue capacity, and the founder's own repository already grades the figure as anecdote |
| Miro commissioned and paid for both rooms | A global collaboration software company commissioned and paid for two rooms. Counter-example: written permission to use the name is not held, and the permission contemplated was for a different business. Name only in private conversation once written permission is held for this use |
| I have the authority and experience of successful events | Describe the record, drop the adjective. Convened and delivered two commissioned rooms, and ran the exhibition programme at a health technology company. Counter-example: successful is unscoped, and the same record includes three accepted guests who did not appear and a room cancelled four days out |
| AI content is eroding trust, so events are becoming necessary | Trust in digital channels is measurably declining and the cost of reaching a buyer through them is rising. In-person contact is one of the channels that benefits. Counter-example: the share of machine-written articles has been reported as level since mid-2024, the trust surveys measure consumers rather than B2B buyers, and the causal chain from one to the other is asserted rather than shown |
| In-person events have more trust and more conversion | B2B organisations report events as their highest-confidence channel and report in similar numbers that they cannot attribute the return. The gap between the two is the opening. Counter-example: belief is not measurement, and the conversion figures available for executive dinners come from vendor marketing rather than research |
| It will keep getting harder and more expensive to get leads online | State the observed trend with its dates and stop. Counter-example: at least one published benchmark forecasts median paid acquisition cost falling in 2027. A forecast stated as a certainty is falsifiable by a prospect who has read the other forecast |
| We bring you attendees from your ideal customer profile | A stated attendance floor with a named remedy, in contract language. Counter-example: free-to-guest events lose a large share of registrants, the strongest predictor of attendance is payment, and the guest here never pays. The promise has to be a number with a consequence attached or it is not a promise |
| The Warm Channel, implying a network that already exists | The name describes the method, not an existing asset. Counter-example: there is no guest list in this vertical yet, and warmth is manufactured per engagement in year one. Do not write copy implying a standing network until one exists |

## Hard bans in every phase

Do not use, anywhere in the run: any client count, any attendee total, any named client, any testimonial, any quoted praise, any logo, any conversion, meeting, pipeline or revenue figure, any guarantee of an outcome beyond attendance, any unsourced market statistic, any claim of an existing standing guest network, and any use of the healthcare guest list or the healthcare business's references.

The adjacent outcome this buyer will assume unless it is refused in plain words is pipeline. Refuse it explicitly. Paid on who is in the room, and what happens in the conversation belongs to the client.

# Copy notes

What the page does, why it is shaped this way, and what is still open.

## The shape changed, not just the words

Previous versions were written as an essay: arguments in document order, a
refusals table, and a six question FAQ. That reads as a position paper. A
position paper invites a reader to consider, weigh and come back later.

This version runs the `landing-page-copywriter` 14-section anatomy, which is a
conversion sequence rather than an argument:

| Formula block | What it does here |
|---|---|
| Hero | Names the transformation and identifies the reader |
| What your pipeline desperately needs | Three needs, framed deeper than symptoms |
| What you're already doing right | Four validations, which earn the right to the next block |
| But you're still | Five pains, each mirroring a validation |
| Crescendo | One high-emotion transition, no heading |
| The solution | Positioning, with the "this isn't X, this is Y" line |
| The big promise | The one line the rest of the page proves |
| How it works in 3 steps | The mechanism, made to feel achievable |
| Beyond the 3 steps | Measurement, the only real extra capability |
| vs the alternatives | Comparison, cost table, calculator |
| Transform closing | Old way against new way |
| CTA block | Founding client terms |

**Blocks 3 and 4 are a single mechanism.** The pain only lands because the
validation came first. Anyone editing this page for length will be tempted to
cut block 3, because on its own it looks like flattery. Cutting it turns block
4 into an accusation.

## Two sections of the formula were deliberately dropped

The skill treats both as always present. They are not here, on instruction.

**No FAQ.** Every question answered on a page is a question the reader was not
necessarily asking, and answering it invites them into a deliberation rather
than an action.

**No "what we refuse to do".** It was the strongest block on the earlier page
and it is still true, but it belongs in a scope document and a sales call, not
on a page whose only job is a booked call. The exclusions are commercially
important and they survive in `run-a-client/phase-9-pricing-packaging.md`.

**One call to action, repeated.** Book a call, three times, identical wording.
No scorecard, no newsletter, no pricing table, no lower-commitment secondary.
A second option is a way of not taking the first one.

## The sourcing problem, unchanged and still yours to close

Most of the channel economics on this page are grade C plus: agency blogs and
benchmark aggregators, not named institutions with published methods. CEIR and
Instantly are the only B grades.

`GTM-BRIEF.md` Section 5 says grade C never enters a client document. It is
used here anyway, because the page is argued on lead generation economics and
those are the only figures that exist at that granularity.

**One hour of work closes this.** Pull your own historical cost per lead and
cost per meeting, and check the table against them. Your own numbers outrank
every aggregator on this list, and they convert a grade C page into a grade A
one.

## What the page does not claim

Nothing on this page states a Warm Channel conversion rate, lead count, meeting
count, pipeline figure or revenue figure. There is no delivered engagement to
support one.

The big promise, that every lead has already met you in person, is a
**structural** claim rather than a performance claim. It is true by the nature
of the channel and does not require a single delivered result to stand behind.
That is why it is the promise: it is the strongest thing that can honestly be
said before the first engagement.

The checkmark list in that block is capability, not performance. Read it again
before editing: the moment one of those lines acquires a number, it becomes a
claim that cannot currently be supported.

The calculator supplies no conversion rate either. Every input is the client's,
and it will return a bad answer when the numbers are bad.

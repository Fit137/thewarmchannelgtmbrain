## The two findings that change the run

Both are uncomfortable and both go in.

**1. Paid-on-attendance is not unique.** The brief's one-liner is that The Warm Channel is "paid on the executives who arrive." At least two operators in slot 1 already do a version of this. Ortus Club is described by clients as "charging only for attendees who show up." MyOutreach states that delegates are "confirmed before billing" and that "no-shows are never charged." The billing model is therefore a category norm among the better operators, not a differentiator, and Phase 3 cannot lead with it. Source is third-party review text and search summaries, so grade it C plus and confirm before any competitive claim is made, but plan as though it is true.

**2. Competitors guarantee more than attendance, and The Warm Channel refuses to.** ConvergeX is reported as guaranteeing that at least 50% of attendees book a post-event meeting, and claiming a current average near 70%. CXO & Co is described as offering guaranteed attendance plus post-event meeting support. This is the adjacent outcome `GTM-BRIEF.md` Section 5 says the buyer will assume unless it is refused in plain words. The buyer is not assuming it out of nowhere. They are assuming it because the category sells it. The refusal is now a harder sell and a sharper position at the same time, and Phase 3, Phase 6 and Phase 9 all have to carry it deliberately.

## The real positioning axis

Step 2.1 said to record first whether an operator sells access to an audience it owns or composition of the client's own list. The slate splits cleanly, and this is the axis the category runs on.

- **Audience they own:** slot 4 entirely, Evanta, Argyle, GDS, Millennium. The sponsor gets access to the operator's community. The account list is the operator's.
- **Client's own named list:** slot 1, the better operators. ConvergeX is described as "mapping target accounts and personally recruiting a table of genuinely senior buyers who match the ICP."
- **The Warm Channel sits on the second.** It is therefore not differentiated against slot 1 on the axis, and is strongly differentiated against slot 4, which is the set a prospect is most likely to name out loud. That is the gap the brief predicted and the research confirms it.

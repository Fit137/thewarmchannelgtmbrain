## The grading problem with this pass, stated plainly

**Most of the channel economics above are grade C plus.** They come from agency blogs and benchmark aggregators rather than from a named institution with a published method. `GTM-BRIEF.md` Section 5 says grade C content is directionally useful and never goes in a client document.

The rewritten landing page uses them anyway, because the founder asked for a page argued on lead-generation economics and those are the only figures that exist at that level of detail. **Every one is attributed on the page itself**, which is the mitigation, not a fix.

Two consequences to accept or reject deliberately:

1. **A prospect who checks the sources will find blog posts, not institutions.** That is survivable if the numbers are in the right range and fatal if they are not. Worth one hour cross-checking the CPL table against your own historical spend before publishing.
2. **Figure 32 is the dangerous one and it is not used as a claim.** The 30% to 50% dinner conversion rate and the 300% to 500% event ROI are the most flattering numbers in the entire research set and they come from companies that sell executive events. They appear on the page only as an optional pre-fill in the calculator, labelled as an industry benchmark and not ours. They must never appear as a headline.

## The tension in the attendance numbers, and how this run resolves it

Figure 6 and figure 11 disagree on the surface. Pheedloop says free events lose 40% to 60% of registrants. Clutch says executive dinners attend at 70%, so they lose 30%.

They are measuring different populations. Figure 6 is free events generally, which includes webinars and large open-registration events where registration costs nothing and means nothing. Figure 11 is invite-only, free-to-attend, executive events, which is exactly the population The Warm Channel operates in, and it separates registered from invited.

**This run uses figure 11 for anything about a composed executive room, and figure 6 only for the general argument that payment predicts attendance.** Figure 12 is the sharper version of the same point and it is the one to use: vendor-owned conferences attend at 19%, invite-only executive dinners at 70%.

**Consequence for open decision 4, the attendance guarantee.** The brief says to model the floor on the worst show rate actually produced rather than the best. There is no Warm Channel show rate yet, so there is nothing to model on, and the floor stays `{TBD}`. What figure 11 does give the founder is the shape of the arithmetic. A 70% rate against confirmations means a floor of 12 in-ICP attendees needs roughly 17 confirmations, and a floor set against invitations rather than confirmations is a different and much riskier promise. Figure 13 helps, because director-and-above is the target title and attends better than the technical population that drags the average down.

None of these figures is The Warm Channel's own performance and none may be presented as such.

## Step 2.7, review analysis, and the gap in it

The skill sends this step to G2. The category is not on G2. The substituted sources were LinkedIn, Reddit and industry forums, trade press, published case studies read for omissions, and conference talks.

### What came back

**Positive themes, thin but real.** Ortus Club is reviewed on Clutch and Trustpilot. The repeated praise is attendee quality, organisation, moderated and pitch-free discussion, and billing flexibility, specifically charging only for attendees who show up. No frequency counts are available without reading the review pages, and the review pages are blocked here.

**Negative themes: nothing.** Multiple search formulations returned no buyer-side complaints about any slot 1 operator. What the searches did surface was employee reviews on Glassdoor, which are about working there and are not buyer feedback, and must not be read as such.

### Why the absence is not a finding

Three explanations, and this pass cannot separate them.

1. The category is small and its buyers are marketers who do not post publicly about vendor performance.
2. Complaints exist on the blocked review pages, behind logins, or in private.
3. There are genuinely few complaints.

A search-only pass that returns nothing supports all three equally. **Recording this as "competitors have no weaknesses" would be the single worst error available in this run,** because Phase 2.8 builds vectors 3 and 4 from the reverse of complaints, and a fabricated complaint becomes a positioning claim two phases later.

### What this does to Phase 2.8

Vectors 1 and 2, what competitors lead in, can be built. Vectors 3 and 4, what The Warm Channel can lead in, cannot be built from complaint data. They are built instead from **structural differences that are observable without reviews**, and every one is marked as structural rather than review-derived in Phase 2. That is a weaker foundation than the skill intends and Phase 2 says so at the point of use.

### The attendee-side research, which did work

The prompt asks separately about the guest side, and this is the most productive part of the pass.

| Question | Finding | Grade |
|---|---|---|
| Is there fatigue with vendor-hosted dinners | **Yes, and it is documented in cybersecurity.** More than 5,000 global cybersecurity vendors. 68% of CISOs report aggressive marketing makes it near impossible to tell genuine innovation from AI-washing. Category commentary states plainly that a CISO can recognise a lead-generation dinner from the subject line | C plus for the commentary, B and A minus for the CISO stress surveys, Nominet and Proofpoint |
| Is there a shift to peer-only formats | **Yes, visible in the product design rather than in survey data.** Bank Director's C-Suite Summit sells private, off-the-record peer exchanges. Pitch-free and peer-led framing is the standard claim across slot 1 | C plus |
| Same fatigue in Canadian financial services | **Not found.** No evidence either way for bank, credit union or insurer executives in Canada | Absent |
| What makes them accept or decline | Partial, and from the operator side rather than the executive side. Named factors that move drop-out: persona, technical audiences worse. Timing, H2 and especially August, October, November. Geography, US no-shows around ten times ANZ. Pitch-style framing. Vendor-led against peer-led speakers. Whether guests are reconfirmed at all | C plus |

### What this does to the niche choice

`RESEARCH-PROMPTS.md` says that if the research contradicts the fintech-over-cybersecurity reasoning, Section 1 needs revisiting before Phase 3.

**It does not contradict it. It confirms the first half and leaves the second half unevidenced.** Cybersecurity saturation and buyer fatigue are real and documented. That fintech is less crowded was not tested and remains an assumption. The niche choice stands, and Phase 3 and Phase 4 may cite the cybersecurity saturation as the reason for avoiding that vertical. Neither may claim fintech is uncrowded, because nobody checked.

### The benchmark that came out of this step

The most valuable single output of the whole research pass, and it came from a competitor's own published report. Clutch Events, 13,155 registrations across 191 invite-only free-to-attend executive B2B events, 2025 into early 2026. Executive dinners attend at 70% of registered. Vendor-owned conferences at 19%. Directors and above at 62%, technical individual contributors at 56% with a no-show rate three times higher.

It is graded and its limits are set out in `evidence-ledger.md`, including where it disagrees with the Pheedloop figure the brief already cleared, and which of the two this run uses.

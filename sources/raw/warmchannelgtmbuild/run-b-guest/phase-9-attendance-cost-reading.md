# Run B, Phase 9 substitute. The attendance cost reading

> **RECONCILE.** The guest side mostly survives the 2026-09-22
> repositioning, because a guest attends a convening whatever the client
> bought. Two things are stale and they are not cosmetic. The guest ICP here is
> a named executive at a Canadian bank, credit union or insurer, which followed
> from the old client ICP and no longer does. And the format is fixed at a
> table of twelve, which the new positioning explicitly refuses to name. The
> two-vocabulary rule, the consent boundaries and the attendance arithmetic are
> unaffected and remain correct.

**Run B has no pricing phase.** The guest pays nothing. `SKILL-ADAPTATION.md` substitutes an attendance cost reading: what the guest gives up, what they get, and what makes the second acceptance more likely than the first. It is the phase that decides whether guest-side inventory compounds or has to be rebuilt every time.

This is the guest-side equivalent of Run A's Phase 9, and it deserves the same seriousness. In Run A the question is what a room is worth to a client. Here it is whether the guest's side of the trade holds up, and if it does not, there is no room to sell.

## What the guest gives up

Priced honestly, because a business that does not know what it is asking for will ask badly.

| What they give | Real cost | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| The evening | Three to four hours including travel, on a weeknight, in Toronto traffic | The largest cost and the one most often understated in the invitation. State the end time |
| Family time or rest | Unquantifiable and often decisive | Slot 4 of the guest competitive set, the calendar and the family, wins most evenings and should be assumed to |
| Reputational exposure | Being seen at a vendor-hosted event, and saying something in front of peers who will remember | Rises with seniority. It is why anchors decline more often, and it is not timidity |
| Attention, in advance | Deciding, diarising, and remembering | Small, and it is where most acceptances quietly decay before the date |
| Candour | Saying what they actually think, which is the thing the room is for | **This is the real ask.** An evening of nobody saying anything true is a wasted evening for everyone, and it is what the format exists to prevent |

## What they get

| What they get | Delivered by | Reliability |
|---|---|---|
| A live question answered by peers who have handled it | The question being genuinely researched | Depends entirely on question selection. This is the highest-variance input in the business |
| Three or four people worth knowing, in their own function and city | Room composition | Good, if the no-vendors rule holds and the titles are right |
| Permission to say something they would not say publicly | The closed format, held | Good, and only as good as the last time it was held |
| A written summary, if they want it | Follow-up | Reliable, and cheap |
| Nothing else. No sequence, no list, no calls | Refusals kept | **The most reliable thing on offer**, because it costs nothing to deliver |

## Whether the trade holds

**For a Level 1 guest, yes, and comfortably.** An evening for three useful contacts and a live question is a reasonable trade for a Director who is building a network.

**For an anchor, it is marginal on the first invitation and good on the second.** A VP at a Big Five bank has more invitations than evenings and no shortage of peers. The first acceptance is bought almost entirely by the quality of the question and by who is already confirmed, which is the cold-start problem. The second acceptance is bought by the first evening having been good, which is cheap.

**The implication for how this business spends its effort.** The expensive thing is the first acceptance from an anchor. Everything after it is comparatively cheap. So effort belongs at the front of the funnel and at question selection, not at scale, and a business that tries to grow the cohort by approaching more people rather than by choosing better questions will find the acceptance rate falling as the volume rises.

## What makes the second acceptance more likely than the first

The whole phase reduces to this table, and this table is the compounding mechanism.

| Factor | Effect on second acceptance | Cost to deliver | Priority |
|---|---|---|---|
| The refusals were kept. No list, no details passed, no follow-up sequence | **Largest single factor.** It is the promise most often broken in this category and the one the guest was most sceptical about | Zero. It is a policy | 1 |
| The evening ended when it was said it would end | Large, and almost never done | Zero | 2 |
| The question was real, and someone in the room had actually solved it | Large | High. It is question research, and it is the scarce skill | 3 |
| The room was what they were told it would be | Large. Overstating the room is the fastest way to lose a guest permanently | Zero, and it costs acceptances at stage 3 to protect | 4 |
| Nobody pitched, including the host | Large, and it is a delivery risk rather than a design choice | Medium. It requires being in the room, which is open decision 7 | 5 |
| The summary arrived, and was good | Medium | Low | 6 |
| They were asked for one nomination, not a list | Medium, and it converts a guest into a supply route | Zero | 7 |
| The next invitation came months later, not weeks | Medium. Over-inviting a good guest is how a cohort is spent | Zero, and it requires discipline | 8 |

**Five of the eight cost nothing.** The guest side of this business is won mostly by not doing things, which is fortunate, because a pre-launch business with `{TBD}` runway cannot buy its way to a cohort.

**Open decision 7 appears here and it matters more on this side than on Run A's.** Factor 5, nobody pitching, requires someone in the room to hold the line when a paying client starts selling from the floor. The founder attending the first three rooms is not a nice-to-have on the guest side. It is how the format survives contact with a client.

## The measure

Not attendance. **Repeat acceptance rate, and nominations per attending guest.**

A cohort of twenty who attended once is a cost that was paid. A cohort of twenty who would come again is the only asset this business has, and it is the thing a client is actually paying for when they buy the second room.

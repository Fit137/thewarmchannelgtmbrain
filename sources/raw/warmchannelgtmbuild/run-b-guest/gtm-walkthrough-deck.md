# SLIDE 1, The Warm Channel, Run B, the guest side

> **RECONCILE.** The guest side mostly survives the 2026-09-22
> repositioning, because a guest attends a convening whatever the client
> bought. Two things are stale and they are not cosmetic. The guest ICP here is
> a named executive at a Canadian bank, credit union or insurer, which followed
> from the old client ICP and no longer does. And the format is fixed at a
> table of twelve, which the new positioning explicitly refuses to name. The
> two-vocabulary rule, the consent boundaries and the attendance arithmetic are
> unaffected and remain correct.

- Run B is the supply side. A named executive at a Canadian bank, credit union or insurer, who pays nothing, is not buying anything, and can say no in four seconds.
- Run A cannot be delivered without Run B. Guest side inventory, not the method, is the asset that compounds, so being second in sequence does not make this second in importance.
- Two substitutions against the skill. There is no pricing phase, because nobody pays, and it is replaced by an attendance cost reading. The buying committee collapses to one person.
- The governing rule for this whole run: client facing language never reaches a guest facing surface. A guest who reads the Run A copy learns they were inventory, and the guest list is the only asset this business has.
- Guest side inventory in fintech starts at zero. The healthcare list is out of scope on consent grounds, not brand grounds, and is never ported.
- So what: everything in Run A is a promise about people who have not agreed to anything yet, so this run is where the business is real or is not.

# SLIDE 2, What the guest is actually offered

- Six items, rated on demand, differentiation against the other options for that evening, our effort, acceptance motion, their time cost, and role in building inventory.
- The question wins the first acceptance. One live problem genuinely shared by everyone in the room, named in the first line of the invitation, set before anyone was invited.
- The room wins it alongside the question. Who else is coming, disclosed by role and institution type before anyone accepts, and disclosed honestly when the room is thin.
- Discretion wins the second acceptance, and the second is worth more. No attendee list circulated, no contact details passed to anyone including the host, no follow up sequence.
- Discretion is also free. Every promise in it is a refusal the business already makes in the Run A scope for commercial reasons.
- So what: the guest offer is mostly a set of things that will not happen, which is exactly what a pre-launch business with nothing to show can credibly offer.

# SLIDE 3, What we are competing with for that evening

- Five slots. A competing vendor's dinner, an industry association event, a peer only network with no vendor present, their own calendar and family, and a webinar that costs nothing to skip.
- The calendar and the family is the real competitor and it is undefeated most evenings. Every guest facing decision is read against going home, not against another dinner.
- The peer only network is the format they actually want. Where one exists in their function, it wins. Where it does not, that gap is the opening.
- Vendor event fatigue is documented in cybersecurity, where more than five thousand vendors compete and 68% of CISOs report that aggressive marketing makes genuine innovation hard to identify.
- No equivalent evidence was found for Canadian banking, credit union or insurance executives. That is an absence of data, not evidence that the fatigue is absent, and it should be assumed present until someone asks.
- So what: the competitive question is not which dinner they pick, it is whether the question is worth an evening at all, so question selection is the acquisition strategy.

# SLIDE 4, Positioning, guest facing

- Against another vendor's dinner: not an evening with a sponsor, an evening with the people dealing with the same thing you are. No stage time, no deck, no pitching from the floor.
- Against an association event or a conference: not a room of two hundred, a table of twelve on one question. Nobody is on a panel and nobody is presenting.
- Against staying home: one evening, a known start and a known end, and nothing asked of you afterwards.
- Words that never appear on a guest facing surface: composed attendance, access, inventory, target account, show rate, attendance floor, coverage.
- The trust in digital channels argument is a client argument only. Said to a guest it tells them their own professional presence is noise and their evening is a workaround for someone else's marketing problem.
- So what: two vocabularies, kept apart deliberately, because the only thing that ends this business quickly is a guest reading the client copy.

# SLIDE 5, Who we need first

- Level 1 is the accessible guest, Director or senior manager in payments, risk, digital, data or technology. Higher acceptance rate, fills the room, becomes an anchor in two years.
- Level 2 is the anchor, VP and above at an institution the others recognise. Low acceptance rate, expensive to reach, and one acceptance converts several Level 1 acceptances that would not otherwise have happened.
- Acceptance runs downhill and does not run uphill. A Director is reassured by an anchor's presence. An anchor is not reassured by three Directors.
- So the sequencing inverts Run A. Run A sells to its harder buyer first for confidence reasons. Run B must secure anchors first for mechanical reasons.
- The target cohort is twenty executives across at least three functions, with four to six anchors. Twenty in one function serves one client. Twenty across three serves several.
- So what: inventory that serves one client is a contract, and inventory that serves several is a business, so the cohort is built across functions from the first room.

# SLIDE 6, Buying Committee, collapsed

- The guest is user, manager and decision maker in one person. No budget, no procurement, no approval. The table collapses to one column and is filled honestly rather than padded to three.
- The decision takes under a minute and usually happens on a phone.
- Every reason to decline is about exposure. I will be pitched, my name will end up on a list, it will be twenty people and a panel, I do not know who else is going.
- Every one of those is answered by a refusal rather than by a benefit, and every refusal is already in the Run A scope for commercial reasons.
- The hardest question is how you got my name, and the compliance answer and the acceptance answer are the same answer, which is a reason to get the consent position properly rather than minimally.
- So what: the effort that would go into a committee in Run A goes instead into the founding guest cohort, which is the most valuable single artifact in either run.

# SLIDE 7, The guest journey

- Seven stages, from the question registering as real through to a second acceptance. Stage 7 is the only one that compounds.
- Stage 2, assessment of the sender, is where most of this fails and it fails silently. An unsolicited approach to a senior person at a regulated institution, from an unregistered sender with no institutional standing, gets deleted.
- Stage 3 requires disclosing a thin room honestly. It loses acceptances now and keeps the cohort. Overstating the room wins one evening and ends the relationship on arrival.
- Stage 5 is arrival, not acceptance. The category benchmark for invite only executive events is 70% attending against registered, from 13,155 registrations across 191 events, Clutch Events. It is a benchmark, not a target, and not ours.
- Stage 7 is second acceptance and one nomination. A cohort is guests who returned. Guests who attended once are a cost that was paid.
- So what: the association partnership route exists to skip stage 2 entirely, which is why it is the only route open today.

# SLIDE 8, The question, which is the guest side demand driver

- Run A's demand driver is a scorecard about the client's own list. The guest side equivalent is the question itself, and it is the only thing that earns a first acceptance from someone who owes us nothing.
- A question is live when it is real across institutions, awkward to discuss in public, and someone in the room has genuinely solved it.
- Candidates to verify rather than assume: consumer driven banking implementation, fraud and scam liability, core modernisation sequencing, AI governance inside a regulated function, vendor concentration risk.
- The question can be tested publicly before any approach is spent, by posting it with no invitation attached and no capture form, purely to see whether it lands.
- Question research is the scarce skill in this business and the highest variance input. A business that grows the cohort by approaching more people rather than by choosing better questions will watch its acceptance rate fall as volume rises.
- So what: there is no scorecard, no funnel and no lead magnet on this side, there is one question done properly, and everything else follows from it.

---

# SLIDE 9, Channels

- Three, and all three replace the defaults. No advertising and no sequenced email, ever, on this side. An advertised invitation is a registration page, and it selects for people with free evenings.
- Nomination from confirmed guests is the highest converting route in either run, and it does not exist until one room has happened. One name is asked for, never a list.
- Founder direct approach at low volume and high research is the core method, and it is the same method sold to clients, so the acquisition motion is the only case study available.
- Institutional partnership with the Canadian Bankers Association, the Canadian Credit Union Association, Advocis and Payments Canada borrows the standing the business does not have.
- Two of the three are blocked until the consent position is answered in writing. The third, partnership, is open today and is the slowest.
- So what: the binding constraint on the whole business is a legal question that appears nowhere in Run A's own documents, and Run A cannot be delivered without guests.

---

# SLIDE 10, The attendance cost reading

- There is no pricing phase. The guest pays nothing, so this phase asks whether their side of the trade actually holds.
- What they give: an evening including travel, family time, reputational exposure that rises with seniority, and candour, which is the real ask.
- What they get: a live question answered by peers, three or four people worth knowing in their own function and city, permission to say something they would not say publicly, and nothing else afterwards.
- The trade holds comfortably for a Director. For an anchor it is marginal on the first invitation and good on the second, which is the cold start problem stated precisely.
- Eight factors make a second acceptance more likely, and five of them cost nothing: the refusals were kept, the evening ended on time, the room was what they were told, they were asked for one nomination, and the next invitation came months later rather than weeks.
- So what: the guest side is won mostly by not doing things, which is what makes it affordable for a business with {TBD} runway, and repeat acceptance rate is worth more than anything Run A measures.

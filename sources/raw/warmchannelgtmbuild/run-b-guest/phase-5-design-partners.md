## Step 5.1. The committee, which is not a committee

The guest is user, manager and decision maker in one person. No committee, no budget, no procurement, no approval. **The table collapses to one column and is filled honestly rather than padded to three.**

| Attribute | The guest, all three roles at once |
|---|---|
| Possible job titles | VP or Director of Payments, Risk, Digital, Data, Technology, Operations, Fraud, or Innovation, at a Canadian bank, credit union or insurer |
| Responsibility area | Owns a function inside a regulated institution. Time is the constraint, not budget |
| Purchasing influence | None, and the word does not apply. They decide alone, in under a minute, usually on their phone |
| Core journey stages | All of them, and mostly in one decision |
| Potential reasons to block | I will be pitched. My name will end up on a list. It will be twenty people and a panel. I do not know who else is going. It is a Tuesday in November and I have three of these |
| Drivers | The question is one they are actually dealing with. Someone they respect is going. They get to say what they think without it being repeated. They learn how peers solved it |
| Assets to get buy-in | The invitation itself, and the invitation is the entire asset. Plus a nomination from a peer, which outperforms everything else by a distance |
| Channels to find them | Nomination from a confirmed guest. Founder direct approach. Institutional partnership through slot 6 associations |

**One row does the work.** Reasons to block are all about exposure: being sold to, being listed, being on display. Every one is answered by a refusal rather than by a benefit, and every one of those refusals is already in the Run A scope for commercial reasons. The offer to the guest is mostly a set of things that will not happen.

## The cohort target, and what it is worth

**Twenty named executives who will accept a first invitation.** Concretely, from the ICP split in `phase-1-4-offer-icp-positioning.md`:

- **4 to 6 anchors**, VP and above at recognised institutions, secured first.
- **14 to 16 Level 1 guests**, Director level, built around the anchors.
- **Across at least 3 functions**, so the cohort can support more than one question and therefore more than one client.

**Why across functions matters, and it is the point most likely to be missed.** A cohort of twenty payments executives serves exactly one kind of client. A cohort of twenty spread across payments, risk and data serves three, and the invitation universe for the fourth client is built from the same relationships. Inventory that serves one client is a contract. Inventory that serves several is a business.

## How the cohort gets built

Anchor-first, because acceptance runs downhill. Four routes in the order they should be worked.

| # | Route | Method | Yield | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Institutional partnership** | Slot 6 associations, CBA, CCUA, Advocis, Payments Canada. A co-hosted room where the association supplies the question and the standing | Highest quality, slowest | Borrows the institutional standing the business does not have. Also the cleanest consent position, because the association has a member relationship. **The list ownership term has to be settled before the first conversation, because a partner that keeps the list keeps the business** |
| 2 | **Founder direct approach, low volume and high research** | Named individuals, researched properly, approached once, with the question in the first line | Low rate, high quality | The core method. It is also the method being sold to clients, so it has to be done well for two reasons |
| 3 | **Nomination from a confirmed guest** | After a room, ask each guest for one peer who should have been there | **Highest conversion of any route, and it does not exist until after the first room** | This is why the demonstration room in Run A Phase 8 is the highest-value action in either run. It is the only thing that starts this flywheel |
| 4 | **The demonstration room itself** | Run at the founder's own cost. Produces the first cohort, the first reference and the first funnel table together | The unblocking move | Blocked by open decision 5 |

## The consent position, which gates all of it

**Open decision 5 blocks routes 2, 3 and 4, which is the entire cohort build except the partnership route.**

The core activity is unsolicited commercial contact with named executives at Canadian financial institutions. Canadian anti-spam law and privacy law are the operating environment, not a footnote. The question counsel has to answer in writing:

1. What is the defensible basis for a first unsolicited approach to a named executive at a Canadian financial institution?
2. What does implied consent from a published business contact detail actually cover, and for how long?
3. What does it not cover, specifically regarding follow-up and regarding a second unrelated invitation?
4. Does an invitation to a free peer event constitute a commercial electronic message?
5. What record has to be kept of how each name was obtained?

Question 5 is the one that also answers the guest's own suspicion about how their name was obtained, which is a barrier to acceptance in the profile above. **The compliance answer and the acceptance answer are the same answer**, which is a reason to get it properly rather than minimally.

**Route 1 is the only route open today** and it is the slowest. That is an argument for starting it this week, not for waiting on counsel before doing anything.

## What must never be done to this cohort

Not aspirational. Each of these has a specific mechanism by which it destroys the asset.

| Never | Because |
|---|---|
| Port the healthcare guest list | Open decision 6. Those guests consented to invitations from a healthcare convening business. A fintech vendor's dinner is a different sender and a different purpose, and it is a consent breach before it is a brand error |
| Hand a guest's contact details to a client | It is refused in the Run A scope and it is the promise in the guest offer. Doing it once ends the cohort, because guests talk to each other and this cohort is small enough that all of them will hear |
| Circulate the attendee list before or after | Item 5 of the guest offer. It is the cheapest promise to keep and the most expensive to break |
| Let a client pitch from the floor | It converts a guest into an audience and the format into the thing they were avoiding |
| Show a guest the client-facing copy | Section 5 of the brief. They learn they were inventory |
| Count the cohort as an asset in a client conversation before it exists | Section 5 hard ban on claiming a standing guest network. In year one warmth is manufactured per engagement, and the business name describes the method rather than an existing asset |
| Invite an anchor to a room built for a client whose product they would never buy | It spends the scarcest relationship in the business on a room that cannot be repeated |

## The measure of this phase

Not twenty acceptances. **Twenty executives who accepted, arrived, and would accept again.**

The first number is reachable with persistence. The second is reachable with a good evening. The third is the only one that makes the next client cheaper to serve than the last, and it is the only definition of this business working.

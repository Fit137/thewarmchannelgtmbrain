## Phase 6, step 6.1. Brand promise and positioning, guest-facing

| Element | Definition |
|---|---|
| Brand Promise | An evening with a small number of people dealing with the same problem you are, on a question set before anyone was invited |
| Positioning | A closed room, no stage time, no pitching from the floor, no list circulated and no follow-up sequence. One evening, a known start and a known end, and nothing asked afterwards |

## Step 6.2, Table 1. Pillars

| Value Prop | Description | RTB 1 | RTB 2 | RTB 3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **VP1. **VP3 is the pillar that wins the second acceptance**, and the second acceptance is what builds inventory. It is also the cheapest pillar to deliver, because every promise in it is a refusal the business has already made for commercial reasons in the Run A scope.

## Tables 2 to 10. Copy drills, guest-facing

**A deviation, recorded rather than hidden.** The skill's copy drill has a Headline (Social Proof) field and a Supporting Proof Point field. For a guest, social proof is who else is in the room, and there is no cohort yet, so it is `{TBD}` in all nine. A proof point aimed at a guest is a category error in any case: a guest is not evaluating a supplier, they are deciding about an evening. Both fields are marked and neither is filled.

**A second deviation.** The entire guest-facing copy surface is three messages: the invitation, the reconfirmation, and the note afterwards. Nine drills is more copy than the surface can hold. All nine are written because the skill asks for them, and the mapping to real surfaces is given at the end.

## Phase 7. Guest journey

Seven stages. Stage 7 reads as accepting a second invitation, which is the only measure of whether the room was any good.

### Critical success factors

1. **Stage 2 is where most of this fails, and it fails silently.** An unsolicited approach to a senior person at a regulated institution from an unregistered sender with no institutional standing is a message that gets deleted. The partnership route exists to skip this stage, which is why it is the only route open today.
2. **Stage 3 honesty, including when it hurts.** Disclosing a thin room loses acceptances now and keeps the cohort. Overstating it wins one evening and ends the relationship on arrival.
3. **Stage 7 is the whole business.** A cohort is guests who returned. Guests who attended once are a cost that was paid.

### Primary journey blockers

1. **The consent position, open decision 5.** It blocks stages 1 and 2 for every route except the partnership one. First on the list for the same reason the tax number is first in Run A: it is infrastructure, and no amount of good work downstream survives its absence.
2. **No institutional standing at stage 2.** No entity, no website, no prior room in this vertical. The partnership route borrows standing. Nothing else supplies it.
3. **Thin rooms at stage 3.** The first room has no confirmed guests to describe, which is the cold start. The demonstration room exists to pay that cost once, deliberately, rather than pay it on a client's engagement.
4. **The client in the room at stage 6.** A host who pitches from the floor converts every guest at the table into a person who will not return, and does it in one evening. This is a delivery risk, and it is managed by contract and by being in the room.

### Acceleration opportunities

1. **Nomination after the first room.** The highest-converting route in the whole plan, and it does not exist until one room has happened.
2. **The association partnership.** Skips stage 2 entirely.
3. **The demonstration room.** Collapses stages 1 to 7 for a whole cohort at once, at the founder's own cost, with no client to disappoint if it goes badly.

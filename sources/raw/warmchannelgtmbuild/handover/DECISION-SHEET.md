## 4. The attendance guarantee, its floor, its remedy and the assumed show rate

| | |
|---|---|
| **The decision** | A named minimum of in-ICP attendees, what the remedy is if it is missed, and what show rate the floor is calculated against |
| **Blocks** | Phase 6 drill 5, which is the strongest copy in Run A and is currently a template. Phase 9 cannot quote The Room. Phase 2 vector 4 scores 7.6 as designed and nearer 4 until this settles |
| **Brief's proposed default** | A named minimum with a credit or a re-run as the remedy, modelled on the worst show rate actually produced rather than the best, and never a meeting or pipeline guarantee |
| **What the run found** | There is no Warm Channel show rate to model on, so the brief's instruction cannot be followed literally yet. What the research did supply is the arithmetic shape. Invite-only executive dinners attend at 70% of registered on a benchmark of 13,155 registrations across 191 events, Clutch Events. A floor of twelve in-ICP attendees therefore needs roughly seventeen confirmations. **Two things to get right:** the floor must be set against confirmations, not against invitations, because a floor against invitations is a much riskier promise. And directors and above attend at 62% against 56% for technical individual contributors, so the title mix in the room changes the arithmetic |
| **My read** | Set the floor conservatively for the first three rooms and say so openly. A floor you exceed is worth more than a floor you negotiate. The remedy should be a re-run rather than a credit, because a re-run costs founder time the business has and cash it does not |
| **Decision** | |

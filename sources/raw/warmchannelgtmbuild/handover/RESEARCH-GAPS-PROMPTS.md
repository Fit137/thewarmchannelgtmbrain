## Gap 1. Competitor weaknesses, which came back empty

**Why it matters.** Step 2.7 builds two of the four positioning vectors from the reverse of buyer complaints. No buyer-side complaints were found for any operator, in any search formulation. Vectors 3 and 4 in `run-a-client/phase-2-competitive-analysis.md` are therefore built from structural differences instead, and are marked as such. This is the weakest joint in the whole build.

**Do not accept an empty result as a finding.** A search that returns nothing supports three different explanations equally: the buyers do not post publicly, the complaints are behind logins, or there are few complaints. Distinguish them.

```
I am researching buyer complaints about done-for-you executive event and
attendee recruitment agencies. These are agencies that fill B2B executive
dinners and roundtables for corporate sponsors.

Operators to investigate:
1. The Ortus Club, ortusclub.com
2. MyOutreach, my-outreach.com
3. ConvergeX Connections, convergexconnections.com
4. Clutch, clutchgroup.co and clutchevents.co
5. CXO & Co, cxoand.co
6. Executive Leaders Network, elnevents.com

For each, find published BUYER feedback, meaning feedback from the sponsoring
company that paid, not from attendees and not from employees.

Read their Clutch.co and Trustpilot profiles directly and extract:
- Repeated positive themes, with how often each appears
- Repeated negative themes, areas for improvement, and anything a reviewer
  says they wished had been included, with how often each appears

Then look beyond review sites:
- LinkedIn posts and comments by field marketers describing what worked and
  what did not at an agency-run executive dinner
- Reddit and industry forums for B2B marketing and demand generation
- Conference talks where a field marketer describes an agency engagement
- Published case studies, read for what they carefully avoid claiming

IMPORTANT: employee reviews on Glassdoor are about working there. They are not
buyer feedback and must not be reported as such.

If you find no complaints, say so explicitly and tell me which sources you
checked and which were inaccessible. Do not infer that there are no
complaints. I need to know whether the evidence is absent or the buyers are
silent, because these are different facts.
```

## Gap 2. Canadian trade show booth cost, all in

**Why it matters.** This is the strongest line in `run-a-client/phase-9-pricing-packaging.md`, Anchor 2, the alternative comparison. It is the number the buyer already pays and can verify, and it is currently `{TBD}`.

```
I need the all-in cost of exhibiting at Canadian financial services and fintech
conferences in 2026 and 2027, for a vendor selling into banks, credit unions
and insurers.

Start with this document, which is public:
https://www.canadianlenders.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/2026-Canadian-Finance-Summit.pdf

Extract every sponsorship and exhibitor package with its price and what it
includes, plus the attendee profile and attendee numbers.

Then do the same for:
- Canada FinTech Symposium, canadafintechsymposium.com
- Open Banking Expo Canada, openbankingexpo.com/canada
- FinteQC, finteqc.ca
- Any Payments Canada or Canadian Bankers Association event with a public
  sponsorship deck

For each, report: package name, price in CAD, what is included, stand or booth
size if applicable, and the number and seniority of attendees the organiser
claims.

Then estimate the all-in cost beyond the package fee: stand build, travel and
accommodation for the team, collateral, and staff time. Label these clearly as
estimates and say what each is based on.

I want a single defensible figure for "what a vendor spends to exhibit at one
Canadian financial services conference, all in", with the components shown.
```

## Gap 3. Direct competitor site reads

**Why it matters.** Every tagline, sub-tagline and scope line in the Phase 2 competitor summary is `{TBD}`. The table is usable internally and is not usable in a client document.

```
Open each of these sites and read the homepage, the services or solutions
pages, the pricing page if one exists, and the terms and conditions if
published. Do not use search summaries. Read the pages.

1. ortusclub.com
2. my-outreach.com
3. convergexconnections.com
4. clutchgroup.co
5. cxoand.co
6. gdsgroup.com and evanta.com

For each, record verbatim where possible:
- The homepage tagline
- The secondary headline or sub-tagline
- How they describe what is included in the offer
- Who they say fills the room, and whether attendee recruitment is included
  or assumed to be the client's job
- EXACTLY what is guaranteed, and how it is hedged. Attendance guarantees,
  meeting guarantees and appointment guarantees are three different promises.
  Record them separately and quote the hedging language
- What they explicitly refuse to promise
- Who owns the attendee list and attendee data after the event, if stated
- Whether they sell access to an audience THEY own, or composition of the
  CLIENT's own named account list. This distinction is the positioning axis
  for the whole category and is the first thing to record
- Any public pricing

Two specific claims need confirming or correcting, because they are currently
sourced from third-party summaries rather than the companies' own pages:
- That The Ortus Club charges only for attendees who show up
- That ConvergeX guarantees at least 50% of attendees book a post-event
  meeting, against a claimed average near 70%
```

## Gap 4. Live job postings for the in-house alternative

**Why it matters.** Slot 5 in the competitor slate is the in-house hire. The run used salary aggregates, which give the cost but not the scope. The responsibilities list in a real posting tells you what the buyer currently believes they need, and that is the more useful half.

```
Search current job postings for Field Marketing Manager and Event Marketing
Manager roles at fintech, financial technology and financial services vendors
in Canada, with a preference for Toronto and the Greater Toronto Area.

Return the postings themselves, with working URLs. For each:
- Company and whether they sell into banks, credit unions or insurers
- Posted compensation band if stated
- Required years of experience
- The full responsibilities list, verbatim
- Whether running executive dinners, roundtables or field events appears in
  the responsibilities, and how it is described
- Whether the role is new or a backfill, if stated

I also want a read on time to hire: how long these postings stay open, and
whether any have been reposted.

I am using this to price and scope the alternative of hiring someone to do
this work in-house, so the responsibilities list matters as much as the salary.
```

## Gap 5. Does a Canadian operator exist in slot 1 at all

**Why it matters.** The run found no Canadian done-for-you convening agency. Either none exists, which is the market opportunity the whole strategy rests on, or the search did not reach it, which would change the competitive picture materially. These are very different facts and it is cheap to separate them.

```
Find Canadian agencies that recruit attendees for other companies' executive
dinners, roundtables and private B2B events. The service being sold is filling
the room, not producing the event.

Search specifically for:
- Canadian B2B event marketing agencies offering delegate acquisition or
  attendee acquisition as a named service
- Toronto, Vancouver, Montreal and Calgary agencies serving technology or
  financial services vendors
- Canadian field marketing agencies
- Global operators from the slate who have a Canadian office, Canadian staff,
  or published Canadian case studies

Also check whether the global operators (Ortus Club, MyOutreach, ConvergeX,
Clutch, CXO & Co) run events in Canada at all, and if so how often and in
which cities.

If the answer is that no Canada-based operator exists in this category, say so
directly and tell me what you searched, so I can rely on it. This is the
central assumption in a strategy and I need to know whether it is a finding or
a gap.
```

## One assumption nobody has tested, and it is not on the list above

The brief chose fintech over cybersecurity because the cybersecurity executive dinner market is saturated. **The saturation half is now documented.** More than five thousand cybersecurity vendors compete, and 68% of CISOs report that aggressive marketing makes genuine innovation hard to identify.

**The fintech half is assumed.** Nothing in the research pass tested whether executives at Canadian banks, credit unions and insurers are under-invited, appropriately invited or already saturated. Nine dashboards now sit on top of that assumption.

It is not a research-tool question. It is an afternoon of asking six Canadian bank executives what they currently get invited to and how often they accept. Do that before the first room is priced.

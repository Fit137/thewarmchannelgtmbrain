## What the business does

The Warm Channel is a pre-launch, unincorporated, solo-founder service in Toronto. It has no entity, no business number and no clients yet.

The service: a corporate client, typically a financial technology vendor, supplies a list of named target companies. The Warm Channel researches, identifies and contacts named senior executives at those companies, invites them to a small private dinner or roundtable hosted by the client, manages confirmations and attendance, and is paid by the client per executive who attends.

The client hosts and pays the venue directly. The Warm Channel supplies no venue, no catering and no production.

## The activity the opinion is needed on

The core activity is **unsolicited first contact with named individuals at Canadian financial institutions**, by email and by professional social platform message, inviting them to attend a free private event.

Specifics that may bear on the analysis:

- **The recipients** are employees, typically Vice President or Director level, in payments, risk, digital, data, technology and fraud functions, at Canadian banks, credit unions and insurers. They are contacted in their professional capacity.
- **How names and contact details would be obtained:** published company directories, professional social platform profiles, published speaker lists and conference programmes, association member listings where public, and inference of corporate email format. No purchased lists. No scraped personal addresses.
- **What is sent:** an invitation to a free event. There is no charge to the recipient and nothing is sold to them. The event is paid for by a third party, the client, who is named in the invitation and who has a commercial interest in the recipient's employer.
- **Volume:** deliberately low. Tens of contacts per event rather than thousands. Each is individually researched.
- **Follow-up:** currently intended as a single approach with no sequence, and a reconfirmation message to those who accept.
- **Records:** no record-keeping system exists yet. Designing one correctly is part of what this opinion is meant to inform.

## The questions

A written answer to each is what is needed.

1. **What is the defensible legal basis for a first unsolicited electronic approach** to a named executive at a Canadian financial institution in these circumstances, under CASL?

2. **Does an invitation to a free, third-party-sponsored private event constitute a commercial electronic message** under CASL, given that nothing is sold to the recipient but the sponsor has a commercial interest in the recipient's employer? If the answer is fact-dependent, what facts change it?

3. **What does implied consent from a conspicuously published business contact detail actually cover here,** and for how long? Specifically, does the publication of a business email address on a corporate website or a conference programme support an invitation of this kind, and does the relevance requirement in the implied consent provisions extend to an event invitation?

4. **What does implied consent not cover?** In particular: a follow-up message to someone who did not reply; a second invitation to a different event some months later; and an invitation sent on behalf of a different sponsoring client from the one that prompted the first contact.

5. **Under PIPEDA, what are the obligations** in collecting, holding and using the names, roles and business contact details of these individuals, including the business contact information exemption and its limits, and what happens to those obligations when the individuals are held in a standing list maintained across multiple client engagements?

6. **What records must be kept** of how each contact detail was obtained and on what consent basis, and in what form must they be retained to be useful in a complaint or an investigation?

7. **What is required on the message itself**, including sender identification, mailing address and unsubscribe mechanism, for an unincorporated sole proprietor, and does registering a business name change any of it?

8. **Two structural questions specific to how this business is built:**
   - The Warm Channel intends to retain the guest list and the relationships across engagements, rather than transferring them to any client. Does retaining and reusing that list across different sponsoring clients change the consent analysis for the second and subsequent invitations?
   - Where an industry association co-hosts an event and invites its own members, does the association's member relationship supply a consent basis, and does that basis extend to The Warm Channel as an organiser?

9. **Exposure.** What is the realistic penalty range and enforcement posture for a small business that gets this wrong at low volume, and which of the errors above are the ones that actually attract enforcement?

## A boundary the founder has already set

An existing guest list from a separate healthcare convening business is **not** to be used for this business, on the founder's own decision, on the ground that consent given for invitations from a healthcare convenor does not extend to invitations from a financial technology vendor's dinner. Confirmation that this reading is correct, or correction of it, would be useful.

## What the opinion is for

It determines whether two of three planned acquisition channels can operate at all, and it determines the design of the contact record system before that system is built rather than after. A conservative, written position is more useful than a permissive verbal one.

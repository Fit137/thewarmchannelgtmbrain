# Positioning

Supersedes the offer definition in `GTM-BRIEF.md` Section 3 and the ICP in
Run A Phase 4. Written 2026-09-22 after the room-centric positioning was
rejected. Nothing gets rebuilt until this document is agreed, because three
direction changes have already been paid for.

## What the product is

**The Warm Channel is a done-for-you B2B lead generation channel built on
in-person events.**

Strategy, execution, the people in the room, and the work afterwards that turns
them into sales-qualified leads. All of it. The client runs their business; we
run the channel.

The channel happens to be in-person, which spans many formats: an executive
dinner, a roundtable, a hosted breakfast, a summit, a side event at a
conference, a suite at a trade show, a private session inside an exhibition.
**The format is a delivery detail and it never appears in the headline.** A
vendor planning a 200 person summit and a vendor planning a table of ten are
both customers.

### What this is not

- **Not attendance.** Attendance is one step of four and selling it alone
  undersells the whole. The page never leads with it.
- **Not convening.** Convening is a component, not the product.
- **Not an events agency.** We are not paid to produce an event. We are paid
  for the channel's output.

### The four components, and where they live

Strategy, convening, running the programme, and post-event conversion are
**offer components for the sales call, not website sections.** Putting them on
the page turns one clear outcome into four confusing products and invites the
client to buy the cheapest one. The website sells the outcome. The call
configures the components.

## The dream outcome the page sells

**Highly qualified B2B sales leads that no other channel can produce, at the
moment every other channel is degrading.**

Not leads. Not MQLs. Not meetings. Sales-qualified leads from people who met a
human being, in a room, for long enough to form a view.

The AI argument is the tailwind and it is already evidenced in
`research/evidence-ledger.md`: cold email reply rates fell from 8.5% in 2019 to
3.43% in 2026 while AI tools pushed four to seven times the volume into the
same inboxes. Every channel that can be automated is being automated into
worthlessness. The one that cannot is the one where a person has to physically
turn up.

**The company name was built for this positioning.** A warm channel is exactly
what this is: a lead generation channel whose output is warm. It was never a
name about rooms.

## The ICP

Defined by characteristics, not geography. The service is deliverable
anywhere. **The page never names a country or a city.**

Five attributes, in priority order:

1. **High average contract value**, six figures and up, so the arithmetic of a
   composed audience works at all.
2. **Already spends materially on events.** The budget line exists and the
   belief exists. We are not selling them on the channel, we are selling them a
   better return from a channel they already fund.
3. **The buyer is structurally unreachable cold.** If a sequence can reach
   them, the cheapest channel wins and it is not us.
4. **They already understand warm against cold**, and what the difference does
   to close rate and cycle length. No education required.
5. **Their current event spend is converting badly**, which is the cost of the
   problem, and it is large because the spend is large.

### Where the cost of problem is highest

| Evidence | Grade |
|---|---|
| Trade show exhibiting is 40.8% of exhibitor marketing budgets, the single largest channel line (CEIR 2026 Marketing Spend Decision Report) | B |
| B2B events consume 20% to 30% of the marketing budget | C plus |
| Mid-market reports roughly 1% in-person conversion from trade show leads | C |
| Sponsoring the wrong format guarantees negative return | C, opinion |
| Teams cannot prove event return in a way a CFO accepts, and fall back on badge scan counts | C |

The wedge follows directly: **the money is already being spent, and it is being
spent badly.** This is not a new budget request. It is the same budget,
redirected to produce people who matter.

### Candidate segments

Ranked on the five attributes.

**1. Enterprise cybersecurity vendors.** Recommended.

| Attribute | Fit |
|---|---|
| High ACV | Yes, six and seven figures |
| Spends on events | Heavily. RSA and Black Hat are category fixtures |
| Unreachable cold | The strongest case anywhere. CISOs reportedly field around 60 cold approaches a week and reject most within seconds (grade C) |
| Understands warm vs cold | It is the market's own doctrine. Security buys on peer reference |
| Current spend converting badly | Practitioner view that big-booth return "is just not there," while targeted meeting programmes before, during and after an event do produce pipeline (grade C, opinion) |

That last line is a third party describing this product without knowing it
exists.

**2. Healthcare and life sciences technology selling into health systems.**

High ACV, long cycles, conference-gated buying, relationship-driven. Slightly
weaker than cybersecurity on unreachability, and stronger on one thing that is
not on the list: **the founder ran the exhibition programme at a health
technology company.** That is the only segment where there is real operating
history to stand on, which matters more than usual for a business with no
delivered engagement.

**3. Financial infrastructure and fintech selling into banks and insurers.**

What the current documents assume. Still a good fit: high ACV, regulated,
event-heavy. It ranks third because a VP at a bank is marginally more reachable
than a CISO, so the cost of the problem is a little lower.

### The recommendation, and the honest caveat

**Cybersecurity scores highest on the criteria. Healthcare is where the founder
can already prove something.** Those point in different directions and the
choice is a judgement about whether evidence-of-fit or evidence-of-founder
matters more in the first six months with no case study.

Most of the segment evidence above is grade C: agency content and practitioner
opinion, not named institutions with published methods. The CEIR budget figure
is the only B. This is the same sourcing problem already recorded in
`landing-page/COPY-NOTES.md`, and picking an ICP on grade C evidence is a
bigger bet than writing a page on it.

## What still has to be decided

1. **Which segment.** Cybersecurity on the criteria, healthcare on founder
   proof.
2. **What the channel's unit of value is called**, now that "attendee" is a
   downsell and "lead" is what the buyer actually wants. It has to be nameable
   because the pricing model attaches to it.
3. **Whether pay-per-arrival survives.** It is format-agnostic and honest, but
   it prices a step rather than the outcome, which is exactly the downsell
   problem one level down. Pricing on qualified leads would match the promise
   and would also put delivery risk on a business with no show-rate history.

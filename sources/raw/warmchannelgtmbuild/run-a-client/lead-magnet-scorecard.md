For ScoreApp.com. Built from the demand driver in Phase 1, step 1.6, and appearing as a top-of-funnel asset in Phase 8 channel 1.

**A note on the scale direction.** The skill's template maps a high score to a strong CTA. Here the scale runs the other way, because what is measured is readiness, and a low score is the problem. The tier table below is ordered to match, so the strongest CTA sits at the bottom of the range rather than the top.

**A note on consent, and it is the reason this asset exists.** Someone who completes the scorecard and enters an email address has given express consent to be contacted. Under CASL that makes this the cleanest acquisition channel available to the business, and the only one in Phase 8 that is not gated on open decision 5. Everything in the sequence below is permitted on that basis. **Nothing in this scorecard collects, infers or stores anything about the guest side.** It asks the client about the client's own list.

## Scorecard overview

| Element | Specification |
|---|---|
| Scorecard name | Room Readiness Scorecard. How full would your next executive room actually be? |
| Target ICP | ICP Level 2 first, a fintech or financial infrastructure vendor that has run an owned executive room in the last two quarters and had it underfill. ICP Level 1 second |
| Problem it reveals | Which of two failures they have. **Coverage**, the named list does not contain enough reachable executives in one city to fill a room. Or **access**, it does and they have no way to reach them. Both end in a room set for twenty with eight people in it, and from the inside they feel identical |
| Immediate value to user | A number about their own list that they do not have and cannot easily get, plus the confirmation arithmetic for the room they are already planning |
| Conversion path | Scorecard to result page to the one-title Room Readiness Read, free, to The List, paid, to The Room. The scorecard is the scale version of the read, and the read is what sells the list |
| What it never does | Name an executive, score a guest, promise attendance, quote a close rate, or claim anything about The Warm Channel's own performance |

## Questions

Seven questions. Questions 1 to 3 measure coverage. Questions 4 to 6 measure access. Question 7 measures capacity, which is the Phase 6 VP3 argument and the row that makes the result land without insulting anyone.

| # | Question | Answer options | Scoring | What it reveals |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | How many named target accounts do you have in Canadian financial services? | Under 15 / 15 to 40 / 41 to 100 / Over 100 / We do not have a named list | 0 / 8 / 14 / 16 / 0 | Whether there is anything to compose from. A "no named list" answer is a disqualification and the result page says so kindly |
| 2 | What share of those accounts have a senior decision maker based in the Greater Toronto Area? | Under 20% / 20 to 40% / 41 to 70% / Over 70% / Do not know | 2 / 8 / 13 / 16 / 3 | Geographic density. This is the single most common silent reason a room underfills, and most buyers have never checked it |
| 3 | For the title you are targeting, how many named individuals across those accounts can you currently identify? | None / 1 to 10 / 11 to 30 / Over 30 / Do not know | 0 / 5 / 11 / 15 / 2 | Whether the universe is large enough to survive a 70% attendance rate. Under thirty named individuals, a room of twelve is arithmetically unlikely |
| 4 | In the last twelve months, how many of those named executives replied to an outbound approach from your team? | None / 1 to 3 / 4 to 10 / More than 10 | 0 / 5 / 11 / 15 | Access, measured rather than assumed. Most answers here are lower than the buyer expects and this is usually the question that changes their mind |
| 5 | Have you hosted your own executive dinner or roundtable in the last two years? | No / Yes, it filled / Yes, it underfilled / Yes, we cancelled it | 6 / 14 / 6 / 2 | ICP level, and the emotional state of the buyer. A cancellation is the strongest buying signal in the whole scorecard |
| 6 | If you ran one, what share of the seats you planned were filled on the night? | Did not run one / Over 90% / 60 to 89% / 30 to 59% / Under 30% | 6 / 15 / 10 / 4 / 1 | Their own show rate, in their own words, which is the number the follow-up conversation is built on |
| 7 | Who does the outreach for an event like this today? | Nobody, we send invitations and hope / A marketing generalist alongside other channels / A dedicated field marketer / An agency / Our SDR team | 1 / 4 / 12 / 10 / 6 | Capacity. **The result page must read this as a resourcing fact and never as a competence judgment**, per Phase 5 |

Maximum 100. No question asks for the account list itself, because the free read is where that exchange happens and asking too early kills completion.

## Results tiers

| Score | Tier | Message | CTA |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 to 39 | **Room at risk** | Your next room is unlikely to fill from this list as it stands, and the reason is specific. Your answers point to `{coverage or access, branched}`. Filling twelve seats needs roughly seventeen confirmations, and the universe you described does not currently support that. This is arithmetic, not a judgment on the invitations | Strong. Book the free Room Readiness Read on one city and one title. Say which room you are planning and when |
| 40 to 69 | **Fillable, with work** | The list can support a room, and getting there is a sustained outreach cycle rather than an invitation send. Your answers suggest the gap is `{coverage or access, branched}`. The question is whether anyone has weeks free before your date | Medium. Get the one-page confirmation arithmetic for your planned room size, and the read if you want it against your real list |
| 70 to 100 | **Ready** | Your list has the density and the reach to fill a room. Your constraint is capacity, not composition. Nothing here suggests you need help finding the people. You may need help contacting thirty of them over six weeks | Soft. Take the confirmation arithmetic. Come back when you have a date and no one to run the outreach |

**The branch on coverage against access** is the whole intelligence of the scorecard. Sum questions 1 to 3 for coverage and 4 to 6 for access, and name whichever is weaker in the result message. A buyer told "your problem is that only four of your fifty accounts have a GTA decision maker" has learned something. A buyer told "your score is 42" has not.

**What the result page must not contain.** No Warm Channel performance figure. No testimonial. No client logo. No close rate. No claim that a room run by The Warm Channel would have filled. The 70% attendance benchmark may appear, attributed to Clutch Events with its sample size, described as a category benchmark.

## Post-scorecard email sequence

Four emails. Express consent obtained at completion. Every email carries an unsubscribe and the sender's physical mailing address, which requires open decision 1 to be settled, so **the sequence cannot go live before the entity exists.**

| # | Day | Subject line | Content focus | CTA |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0 | Your room readiness result, and the one number in it that matters | Result delivery and interpretation. Name the weaker of coverage or access. Give the confirmation arithmetic for their stated room size. No selling | Reply with the date of the room you are planning |
| 2 | 2 | Twelve chairs, seventeen confirmations | The arithmetic in full, with the Clutch benchmark attributed. Why the gap between invited and arrived is the whole job, and why it is a capacity problem rather than a skill problem | Book the free read on one city and one title |
| 3 | 5 | The two failures that look identical from the inside | Coverage against access, explained properly. What each one costs. What fixes each one. Introduces The List as the paid artifact, without a price | See what the read produces |
| 4 | 8 | Last one from me | Final note. States plainly what The Warm Channel does not promise: no meeting count, no pipeline figure, no attendee data. Offers the read once more and then stops | Book the read, or reply no and the sequence ends |

**Email 4 is the Phase 6 drill 6 argument, and it is deliberately placed last.** The refusal is the strongest thing this business can say to a buyer who has been promised an outcome before, and it works better as a closing statement than an opening one.

**No email in this sequence may contain** a case study, a testimonial, a named client, an attendee total, a conversion figure, or the founder's prior record in any form. The permitted wording in Section 5 exists for conversations, not for a cold nurture sequence, and four of those claims are gated on artifacts that do not exist.

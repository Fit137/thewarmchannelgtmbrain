## The competence trap, and the only way through it

The single most important row above is Potential Reasons to Block, for the User and the Manager.

The person who most needs this bought is the person it implicitly criticises. A purchase made over their head does not get refused, it gets slow-walked: the list arrives late, the venue is not confirmed, the date moves twice, and the engagement dies in scheduling with nobody having said no.

**Sell capacity, never competence.** The failure being fixed is arithmetic, not skill. Filling a room of twenty senior executives from a named list requires sustained outreach against people who do not reply, over weeks, and no marketing generalist with six other channels to run has those weeks. That is a resourcing fact and it is true of good marketers. The Clutch benchmark helps here: executive dinners attend at 70% of registered, so a room of twelve needs roughly seventeen confirmations, and getting seventeen confirmations from named executives is the work. Nobody underperformed. The arithmetic was never done.

Phase 6 inherits this directly. No pillar, headline or body line may imply the buyer's team did this badly. Three of the nine copy drills are written against the capacity argument for that reason.

## Step 5.2. Design partner profile, Run A

The first paying clients. For Run A this is a normal early-adopter profile. **The Run B design partner profile, the founding guest cohort, is the more valuable artifact and lives in `run-b-guest/phase-5-design-partners.md`.**

| Attribute | Specification |
|---|---|
| Design partner attributes | Has run an owned executive room in the last two quarters and it underfilled. Has a named GTA account list. Will host, has a venue or can get one. Will accept attendance as the deliverable and say so out loud. Willing to be the first, and knows they are |
| Job titles | Field Marketing Manager, Demand Generation Manager, Director of Field Marketing, at a fintech or financial infrastructure vendor |
| Demographics | Marketing team of 8 to 30. Deal sizes above $100,000. Selling into Canadian banks, credit unions or insurers. Head office anywhere, targets in the GTA |
| Responsibility areas | Owns the events line. Owns or influences the named account list. Reports to a VP who remembers the last room |
| Current tools | An event or registration platform already licensed. A CRM. Possibly an event intelligence layer. Outbound sequencing owned by sales |
| Key pain points | Cannot get named executives to accept. Outbound reply rates falling. Trade show spend producing scans not conversations. A specific recent room that underfilled, with a specific cost |
| Critical triggers | Planning the next room now. Budget cycle. A field marketing hire not approved or not working out. A competitor seen hosting in the same city |
| Goals and motivations | Make the next room work. Keep the programme alive. Stop personally chasing non-repliers |
| Decision criteria | Will the right people actually be there. What exactly is promised and what happens if it is missed. Do we keep the relationship. Can I explain this purchase to my VP without it sounding like an admission |
| Influence level | Recommends. Needs the VP. Can kill it silently, so must be brought along rather than bypassed |
| Barriers to purchase | **No tax number on the invoice**, which is open decision 1 and blocks payment regardless of enthusiasm. No references. Solo supplier. The competence reading. Procurement onboarding for a new vendor |
| Information sources | LinkedIn, peers in other vendors, field marketing communities, the VP's own network |
| Success metrics | In-ICP executives who arrived against the floor. Coverage of the named account list. Whether the VP agreed to a second room |
| Early adopter characteristics | Has been burned by a promised outcome and says so unprompted. Prefers a small promise kept to a large one hedged. Comfortable being a reference later. Will trade a lower price for being first and will say what actually happened |

**What to offer a design partner, and what not to.** Offer a reduced fee, first access to the invitation universe being built in their city, and a written attendance floor once open decision 4 settles. Do not offer equity in outcomes, a meeting count, or exclusivity over the guest list. The guest list is retained under the Section 3 contract term, and a design partner who negotiates that away has taken the only asset the business has.

## Step 2.8. Positioning vectors

**Read this before the table.** The skill builds vectors 1 and 2 from four and five star reviews and vectors 3 and 4 from the reverse of one to three star complaints. The complaint data does not exist. Multiple search formulations returned no buyer-side complaints about any operator in the slate, for the reasons set out in `research/01-findings-steps-1.1-2.1-2.7.md`. Employee reviews were found and are not buyer feedback.

So vectors 3 and 4 below are **structural, not review-derived.** They are differences observable in how the offers are built, not gaps buyers have reported. That is weaker and it is marked at the point of use. When the complaint research is done properly, revisit these two first.

**Vector 3, Access Without Handover.** The client reaches executives it cannot reach itself, without giving up the room, the venue or the host position. This is the vector where The Warm Channel beats both halves of the competitive set at once, and it is the only one. The agencies have the reach and take the room. In-housing and the hire keep the room and have no reach. The hosted-buyer operators have the largest reach and the total handover, the sponsor does not choose who attends. The Warm Channel is the only entry that scores above 8 here, and the reason is the delivery model constraint in Section 1 of the brief, no venue, no catering, no production, which was set for cost reasons and turns out to be the position.

**Vector 4, Promise Discipline.** One promise, attendance, with a floor and a remedy, and an explicit refusal of everything past it. The category's own behaviour makes this differentiating: a hosted-buyer operator contractually binds delegates to a meeting count with a penalty for leaving early, and a slot 1 agency quotes a meeting-booking percentage. **The 7.6 is contingent.** It is the score for the position as designed. Until open decision 4 settles the floor, the remedy and the assumed show rate, the promise is a stated intention and not a promise, and the honest score is nearer 4. Settling decision 4 is what earns this vector.

**A fifth vector was considered and held out.** Local Density, meaning depth in one city and one vertical, is the vector this business should own in year two. It is excluded because The Warm Channel would score near zero on it today, having no guest list in fintech, and scoring it on intent would be the aspirational self-assessment the adaptation warns against.

**What the vector set does not contain,** and the omission is the finding: there is no vector on which The Warm Channel beats ConvergeX at what ConvergeX is for. A buyer who wants a meeting count should buy ConvergeX. Phase 3 has to select the buyer rather than argue with that one.

## Step 2.15. Category summary

**Vector 1, Delivered Certainty.** Leader: hosted-buyer operators, 9.1, with ConvergeX close at 8.9. Impact: certainty is what gets budget approved. Operators who can hand a marketing lead a number their VP will accept win the purchase before craft or fit is discussed. This is the vector The Warm Channel cannot compete on in year one and should not try to.

**Vector 2, Event Craft and Production.** Leader: The Ortus Club, 9.3. Impact: craft is what makes guests accept the next invitation, and guest acceptance is what makes the rooms saleable. Ortus acquires clients by being the operator whose rooms executives will actually attend. It is a guest-side advantage that shows up as a client-side one, which is the same mechanism Run B describes.

**Vector 3, Access Without Handover.** Leader: The Warm Channel, 8.7. Impact: it selects a specific buyer, the vendor that wants the executives and will not give up the room. That buyer is currently being served either by an agency that takes the whole event or by an internal team that cannot reach the people. Both are compromises and neither is named as one, because no one is selling against them.

**Vector 4, Promise Discipline.** Leader: The Warm Channel, 7.6 as designed and nearer 4 until open decision 4 settles. Impact: a buyer burned by a promised outcome that did not arrive is the most reachable buyer in this market, and the only thing that reaches them is a smaller promise kept precisely. Discipline converts the absence of a track record from a disqualifier into the argument, but only if there is a number and a consequence. Without those it is just modesty, and modesty does not close.

## Step 2.16. Positioning summary

The Warm Channel can lead this category in the Greater Toronto Area by holding two positions the incumbents structurally cannot. On Access Without Handover, the business supplies the one thing a vendor's marketing team cannot manufacture, named executives who will come, and returns everything else: the venue, the host position, the guest experience and the room itself. The agencies cannot match this without dismantling the production revenue that funds them, and in-housing cannot match it at all, because software does not produce attendance. On Promise Discipline, the business makes exactly one promise, a floor of in-ICP attendees with a named remedy, and refuses the meeting and pipeline outcomes the category sells. For the buyer who has already bought a promised outcome and watched it not arrive, that refusal is the credential, and it is the only credential available to a business with no delivered engagements.

Customer acquisition follows from that selection rather than from reach. The reachable buyer is narrow, a fintech or financial infrastructure vendor in the GTA that has tried this once and been disappointed, and it is reachable by founder-led approach at low volume, which is also a demonstration of the method being sold. Market growth is guest-side rather than client-side: every delivered room adds named executives to an invitation universe that the next client pays to reach, so capacity compounds while the method stays the same. Defensibility is thin in year one and rests entirely on that universe. The method is copyable in a weekend. The list of Canadian financial services executives who have accepted an invitation and returned for a second is not, and it is the only asset in this business worth protecting. The contract term in Section 3 of the brief, that The Warm Channel retains the guest list, is therefore not a commercial preference. It is the entire defensibility argument, and a client that contracts to own the list is buying the business rather than the service.

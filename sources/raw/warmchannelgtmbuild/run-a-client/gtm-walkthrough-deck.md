# SLIDE 2, Service and Offer Matrix

- Seven service items were rated on six vectors, not two. Client demand and competitive differentiation drive the scatter. Delivery effort, sales motion, ticket size and funnel role are read alongside it.
- The List, a named and verified invitation universe for one city and one title, comes out as the highest value item in the set despite the smallest ticket. It is the lowest effort, it sells in one call with no reference, and it is the only item where a paying client funds the asset that compounds.
- Consented introductions was cut. It scored the worst differentiation in the set because competitors promise more, specifically a quantified meeting count, and it is the pipeline promise the business has to refuse in plain words.
- Facilitation on the day is absorbed as a delivery cost for the first three rooms, not billed. It is a commodity in this category and billing a commodity separately reads as padding.
- The offer is The List as the entry, The Room as the core, The Channel as the expansion.
- So what: the item that acquires customers most cheaply is also the only item that builds guest side inventory, so the cheapest sale and the strategic asset are the same product.

# SLIDE 3, Competitive Market Analysis

- Six competitor slots, and two of them are not companies. In-housing on software the client already owns, and hiring a field marketing manager, are the alternatives that hold most of the buyer's attention.
- The category splits on one axis: whether the operator sells access to an audience it owns, or composition of the client's own named list. Hosted buyer and summit operators sell the first. The better agencies sell the second. The Warm Channel sells the second.
- Two findings changed the run. Paid on attendance is not unique, at least two agencies already bill only for attendees who arrive. And competitors guarantee more than attendance, with one reported as guaranteeing at least half of attendees book a follow up meeting.
- The buyer is not imagining a pipeline promise. They have been quoted one. The refusal has to be argued as a position, not delivered as a shrug.
- Weakness data on competitors is {TBD}. A search only research pass returned no buyer side complaints, and recording that as competitors having no weaknesses would have been the worst available error.
- So what: there is no vector on which The Warm Channel beats the strongest agency at what that agency is for, so acquisition depends on selecting the buyer rather than out arguing the competitor.

# SLIDE 4, Positioning, four vectors

- Vector 1, Delivered Certainty. Competitors lead, scoring near 9. The Warm Channel scores 0.8 today and honestly, because nothing has been delivered.
- Vector 2, Event Craft and Production. Competitors lead, scoring above 9. The Warm Channel scores 1.5, because venue, catering and production are outside the offer by design.
- Vector 3, Access Without Handover. The Warm Channel leads at 8.7. The client reaches executives it cannot reach itself and keeps the room, the venue and the host position. Agencies have the reach and take the room. In-housing keeps the room and has no reach.
- Vector 4, Promise Discipline. The Warm Channel leads at 7.6 as designed, and nearer 4 until the attendance floor and its remedy are settled. Vectors 3 and 4 are structural differences, not gaps buyers reported, and that is weaker ground.
- Projected state, year two, adds a fifth vector, Local Density, meaning depth in one city and one vertical. It is excluded today because the score would be near zero and scoring it on intent would be fiction.
- So what: current state wins a narrow buyer on two structural positions, and projected state only arrives if guest side inventory compounds, which is Run B.

# SLIDE 5, ICP, who we win first

- Level 1 is a vendor that has never run its own executive room. Level 2 is a vendor that has run one, set twenty seats, filled eight, and wants it done properly.
- Level 2 is the better buyer, which inverts the usual reading. It has already made the category sale to itself and paid for it, so the conversation opens on arithmetic rather than persuasion.
- Level 2 also knows the failure was attendance, which is the only thing being sold, and it can price the alternative because it knows what the venue and the catering cost.
- Level 1 requires two sales, first that the format is worth doing and then that this supplier should do it. A pre-launch business cannot afford the first sale, so Level 1 enters through The List rather than The Room.
- The hardest disqualifier is cheap to find in the first call: a buyer who needs a meeting count to report should buy an agency, and should be told so early.
- So what: acquisition targets vendors with a recent underfilled room, because a buyer burned by a promise that did not arrive is the only buyer for whom a smaller promise reads as a credential.

# SLIDE 6, Buying Committee

- User is the field marketing or demand generation lead. Manager is the VP Marketing. Decision Maker is the CMO, with the CRO involved where budget crosses from marketing to sales.
- The User recommends and cannot sign, but can kill the purchase silently. The list arrives late, the date moves twice, and the engagement dies in scheduling with nobody having said no.
- The blocker that matters is the competence reading. The person who most needs this bought is the person it implicitly criticises, and the same reading applies to the VP whose team is being supplemented.
- The answer is to sell capacity and never competence. Twelve seats needs roughly seventeen confirmations, and that is weeks of outreach against people who do not reply. Nobody underperformed, the arithmetic was never done.
- The Manager's unanswered question is what to report upward, because this business refuses to supply the number the category supplies. The attendance floor and the composition report are the answer, and the floor is {TBD}.
- So what: the purchase closes on a document that lets the User explain the decision without it sounding like an admission, so the scope page is a sales asset rather than paperwork.

# SLIDE 7, Customer Journey Funnel

- Seven stages, from a room that underfilled through to a contracted programme. One person is in every row, so the Function column currently reads founder throughout.
- Stage 3 is the hinge. The Room Readiness Read is the only asset that delivers value before payment, needs no track record, and produces a number about the buyer's own business.
- The first blocker is not persuasion. There is no registered business name and no tax number, so there is no invoice a corporate accounts payable department will accept, and the funnel fails at stage 4 after every earlier stage has worked.
- Stage 6 is where the proof set stops being empty. The first client willing to be named is worth more than the second client's fee, because eighteen proof fields in the value proposition work are waiting on it.
- Stage 7 reads as the follow on engagement, the second room and then the annual programme, not a subscription renewal.
- So what: two concurrent engagements is probably the delivery ceiling for one person, so the funnel has a capacity limit before it has a demand limit.

# SLIDE 8, Lead Magnet, the Room Readiness Scorecard

- Seven questions on ScoreApp, scored out of 100. Questions 1 to 3 measure coverage of the named list. Questions 4 to 6 measure access to it. Question 7 measures who is actually free to do the outreach.
- It separates two failures that feel identical from the inside. Either the list does not contain enough reachable executives in one city, or it does and there is no way to reach them. Both end in a room set for twenty with eight people in it.
- The result names the weaker of the two in plain words. A buyer told that only four of their fifty accounts have a Toronto decision maker has learned something. A buyer told their score is 42 has not.
- It converts because the fix for either failure is composition work the client is not resourced to do, and because the next step is a free read on their real list rather than a demo.
- It is also the only acquisition channel not gated on the consent question, because completing the scorecard and entering an email is express consent under Canadian anti spam law.
- So what: this is the one asset that can be built and run today, before the entity, the price bands or a single delivered room exist.

# SLIDE 9, Assets and Collaterals Library

- Three channels, and all three replace the defaults. High volume cold email and paid ads were removed, because a business arguing that cold digital outreach is decaying cannot credibly run it at volume, and paid against an unproven offer buys expensive evidence.
- Channel 1 is founder led organic and low volume direct approach. The acquisition motion is the method being sold, so it demonstrates the product.
- Channel 2 is a demonstration room, run at the founder's own cost, with prospective clients invited to observe. It produces the first reference, the first guest cohort and the first auditable funnel table in one exercise.
- Channel 3 is partnerships with Canadian financial services associations and with providers already selling to fintech vendors. Borrowed credibility, and a second route to guest side supply.
- The demand driver appears at top of funnel in all three: as a public composition teardown, as the room itself performed rather than written, and as a category read for a partner's own membership.
- So what: the highest value channel is the demonstration room and it is entirely blocked by the consent question, so the legal position is the acquisition plan rather than a footnote to it.

# SLIDE 10, Pricing and Packaging

- The value metric is the attending in-ICP executive. It is verifiable by both parties on the night, and it makes the three scopes comparable to each other.
- The secondary metric is the standing invitation universe, per city and per title. It applies to The Channel, and it is the metric that describes the asset rather than the event.
- Per outcome pricing on meetings or pipeline is rejected absolutely. Pricing on the client's pipeline is an outcome claim with a payment schedule attached.
- Three scopes. The List as the entry and the real decoy, The Room as the hero priced per attending executive against a floor, The Channel as the anchor. Venue, catering and production are passed through and contracted by the client directly.

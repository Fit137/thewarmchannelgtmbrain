# Run A, Phase 1. Service and Offer Matrix

Rebuilt 2026-09-22 against `POSITIONING.md`. The previous version scored a
product that is no longer the product: it treated one composed room as the
offer and ranked seven delivery items around it. The offer is now the channel
itself, and the items below are its components.

Six vectors per `SKILL-ADAPTATION.md`. Steps 1.3 to 1.5 run on the first two,
vectors 3 to 6 are read alongside the scatter.

## The item set

The channel is sold as one thing. These are what it is made of, and what a
sales call configures.

| # | Component | What it is |

| 2 | **Audience build** | Finding the right individuals at the named accounts and getting them to commit |

## What the two tables say together

**Item 4, post-event conversion, is the item that turns this from an events
service into a channel.** It has near-top demand, real differentiation, and it
is the step practitioner reporting says the category skips. It is also the only
item that produces the thing the page promises. Dropping it to reduce delivery
effort would collapse the positioning back into the room business that was just
rejected.

**Item 6, measurement, is the sleeper.** The research is consistent that
marketing teams cannot prove event return in a form a CFO accepts and fall back
on badge scan counts. An operator who runs the channel and reports it in
pipeline terms removes the single biggest reason event budgets get cut. It is
low effort and it is what gets a second engagement funded.

**Item 3, programme running, is kept but never priced separately.** Commodity
competitiveness, effort 7, non-scaling. It is carried as a delivery cost because
the conversation design is what makes attendance worth having, and a commodity
billed separately reads as padding.

**Item 5, the coverage read, stays a diagnostic and is not a product.** Unique
with almost no demand. It is the no-reference-needed way to open a conversation,
which matters enormously for a business with no delivered engagement, but it is
a door and not a room.

**The offer that comes out of Phase 1:** one bundled channel engagement,
comprising strategy, audience build, running and post-event conversion, wrapped
in measurement. The coverage read is the free or low-cost entry. Nothing is
sold as a fragment on the website, because a buyer offered four components buys
the cheapest one and then judges the channel on it.

## Step 1.6. The demand driver

| Attribute | Description |
|---|---|
| What it is | **The Channel Coverage Read.** The client sends its named target account list. It comes back with how many of those accounts have a reachable decision maker at the target title, which events those people already attend, and what a realistic composed audience drawn from that list would look like |
| Immediate value | A number the client does not have. Most target account lists have never been tested for whether the people on them can be assembled at all |
| How it helps in problem identification | It separates two failures that feel identical from inside. Either the list does not contain enough reachable people to compose an audience, which is a coverage problem, or it does and the client has no way to reach them, which is an access problem |
| How it exposes the need | Neither failure is fixed by a bigger booth or a nicer venue. Both are fixed by the channel work, which the client is not set up to do |
| ICP fit | Exact. It requires a named account list, which is the qualifying attribute, and it is most uncomfortable for the vendor already spending heavily on events with poor conversion |
| Conversion mechanism | Row renamed. The skill's own label uses a word the brief bans. The read ends on a number the client now owns and cannot forget |
| Top-of-funnel use case | Free, fast, and producible before any delivered engagement exists, which is the state this business is in |

## Step 1.7. Demand driver variants

| Variant | Perceived Value | Ease of Delivery | Delivery Type | TOFU use case |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Coverage read, full | High | Hard | Service, white glove | Named priority accounts only |
| Coverage read, one-segment sample | Medium high | Medium | Service, light | The workhorse, offered on the first call |
| Channel economics calculator | Medium | Easy | Light tool | Built. Client supplies every conversion input |
| Event portfolio teardown | Medium high | Medium | Service into content | A public event read for who was actually there. No client list involved, no confidentiality problem |
| Coverage scorecard | Medium | Easy | Self-assessment | The scale version, specified in `lead-magnet-scorecard.md` |

**Selected for build:** the one-segment sample as the human version, the
calculator as the self-serve version. Both already exist in some form.

**Rejected:** anything that promises a lead count, a meeting count or a named
attendee. The read tells the client about the client's own list. It says
nothing about what The Warm Channel can deliver, because nothing has been
delivered yet.

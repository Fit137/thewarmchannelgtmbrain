## Step 9.1. Value metric selection

The skill's five denominators, evaluated against this business.

| Denominator | Correlation to value | Scalability | Ease of measurement | Score | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Per user or per seat | None. Nobody logs in | n/a | n/a | 0 | Not applicable. There is no product to seat |
| Per usage, per invitation sent | Low, and perverse. Sending more invitations is a sign the universe was too thin, not that more value was delivered | High | Easy | 2 | **Reject.** It pays the supplier for the failure mode |
| Per usage, per confirmation | Medium. Closer, but a confirmation that does not arrive is worth nothing to the client | High | Easy | 5 | Reject as the primary. Keep as the internal operating metric |
| **Per outcome, per attending in-ICP executive** | **High. It is the deliverable** | Medium. Capped by room size and by delivery capacity | Easy, and verifiable by both parties on the night | 9 | **Select** |
| Per outcome, per meeting or per pipeline dollar | High correlation to what the buyer wants, and **prohibited** | n/a | n/a | n/a | **Reject absolutely.** Pricing on the client's pipeline is an outcome claim with a payment schedule attached. It is the thing Section 5 refuses, made structural |
| Per capability, per city or per standing universe | Medium. Describes The Channel rather than The Room | High, and it is the version that compounds | Medium | 7 | Select as the secondary, for The Channel only |

**Primary value metric: the attending in-ICP executive.** Real per-unit denominator, verifiable by both parties, and it makes the three scopes comparable to each other.

**Secondary metric: the standing invitation universe, per city and per title.** Applies to The Channel. It is the metric that describes the asset rather than the event, and it is the one that survives when the business stops being a series of rooms.

**The definition that has to be written before anything is priced.** In-ICP is the whole contract. It needs a title list, a seniority floor, an institution list and a named-account test, agreed in writing before outreach starts. A vague in-ICP definition means the supplier decides what counts on the night, which is precisely the behaviour this business exists to be the opposite of.

## Step 9.2. Engagement scopes

Not four subscription plans. Three scopes, plus a deliberate decoy, following the Decoy, Hero, Anchor logic in engagement form.

| Scope | Position | What it is | Duration | Included | Passed through to client | Price | Target mix |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **The List** | Entry, and the real decoy | A named, verified, ranked invitation universe for one city and one buyer title, with a written read on who will accept and why | 2 to 3 weeks | Universe build, verification, ranking, written read | None | `{TBD}` | 30% to 40% of engagements, and most first purchases |
| **The Room** | Hero | One composed room of 12 to 20 seats from the client's named account list | 8 to 10 weeks | Theme design, list build, outreach, confirmation, show-rate management, composition report, written summary | **Venue, catering, production, staffing. Client contracts and pays these directly** | `{TBD}`, priced per attending in-ICP executive against a floor | 50% to 60% |
| **The Channel** | Anchor | Four rooms across twelve months from a standing invitation universe, one to three cities | 12 months | All of The Room, four times, plus universe maintenance and an annual composition review | As above | `{TBD}`, priced on the standing universe plus per-attendee | 5% to 10% in year one |
| Facilitation on the day | Not sold | Founder present and facilitating | Per room | Absorbed inside The Room for the first three rooms | n/a | Not priced | Per open decision 7 |

**Why The List is the decoy and not a loss leader.** It is priced to be worth doing on its own, and its job in the structure is to make The Room look inevitable. A client who buys The List and learns their GTA coverage is thin has been told, by an artifact they paid for, that the room they were planning would have underfilled. The next conversation is not a sale.

**Why there is no free tier.** The Pheedloop finding in the brief holds in both directions: payment predicts commitment. A free composition read attracts buyers who will not host, and hosting is a qualifying requirement.

## Expansion levers

1. **City.** The second city needs a new universe. This is the cleanest expansion and the one The Channel is built for.
2. **Title.** A universe built for Head of Payments does not serve Head of Risk. Each is a separate build against the same institutions.
3. **Frequency.** One room to four. The standing universe is what makes rooms two to four cheaper to deliver and is the margin story.
4. **Rejected lever, and it is the obvious one.** Selling attendee contact data, introductions or the guest list. It is banned in Section 5, it is refused in the Phase 2 presence table, and it would convert the only compounding asset into a one-time payment.

## The contract term that is not a pricing detail

The Warm Channel retains the guest list, the relationships and the intelligence produced. The client receives the room, the host position, the composition report and the summary.

A client that contracts to own the list is buying work for hire. **That version is priced materially higher or declined**, and the reason is in Phase 2: the method is copyable in a weekend and the list is not. Selling the list once is selling the business once.

### The "one deal pays for X years" calculator, and why it is built differently here

The skill asks for a calculator of the form "One outcome at $X pays for Y years of plan Z."

**Built the obvious way, this is prohibited.** It requires a deal size and a close rate, and any close rate The Warm Channel supplies is a conversion claim, which Section 5 bans absolutely and which Phase 6 drill 6 argues against by name. A supplier that refuses to promise meetings and then publishes a pipeline ROI calculator has made the refusal worthless.

**Built honestly, it is still the sharpest tool in the phase.** The client supplies every input. The Warm Channel supplies only the fee and the arithmetic.

| Input | Who supplies it |
|---|---|
| Your average closed deal size in this segment | **Client.** They know it. We never estimate it |
| Your close rate from a senior first conversation | **Client.** We never supply this, in any form, including as an illustration |
| Number of in-ICP executives in the room | The floor, `{TBD}`, from the contract |
| Engagement fee | `{TBD}` |
| Output: deals needed to cover the fee | Arithmetic |

**The line that may be said:** "Put your own deal size and your own close rate in. We will not put a number in either of those boxes, because we do not know them and we are not paid on them."

**The line that may never be said:** any version that contains a close rate, a meeting count, a pipeline figure, or an implied one. Including in an example. Including with the word "illustrative" next to it.

# Run A, Phase 4. Ideal Customer Profile

Rebuilt 2026-09-22 against `POSITIONING.md`. The previous version was bound to
a city. The service is deliverable anywhere, so geography is out of the
definition and out of the copy entirely.

**The page names no country and no city.** Where the founder chooses to
concentrate effort is an operating decision, not a positioning one, and putting
it on the page shrinks the market for no gain.

## Step 4.1. Two-level ICP

### Level 1, the qualifying characteristics

A company is in the ICP when all five hold. These are the definition. The
vertical below is a concentration choice made on top of them.

| # | Characteristic | Why it qualifies | How it is checked on a first call |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **High average contract value**, six figures and up | Below it the arithmetic of a composed audience does not work at any fee | Ask for ACV. They will say it |
| 2 | **Already spends materially on events** | The budget line exists and the belief exists. We are not selling the channel, we are selling a better return from one they fund | Ask which events they exhibited at or sponsored in the last year |
| 3 | **The buyer is structurally unreachable cold** | If a sequence reaches them, the cheapest channel wins and it is not us | Ask what their reply rate on outbound to that title is now |
| 4 | **They already distinguish warm from cold** | No education required, and the value proposition lands without a lecture | Ask what a referred opportunity closes at against a cold one |
| 5 | **Current event spend converts badly** | This is the cost of the problem, and it is large because the spend is large | Ask what last year's flagship event produced in pipeline. Many cannot answer, which is itself the answer |

**Characteristic 5 is the wedge.** Trade show exhibiting is reported at 40.8%
of exhibitor marketing budgets, the single largest channel line (CEIR 2026
Marketing Spend Decision Report, grade B). Mid-market reports roughly 1%
in-person conversion from trade show leads (grade C). The money is already
being spent, and it is being spent badly. This is not a new budget request.

### Level 2, the concentration segment

**Enterprise cybersecurity vendors.** Chosen on the criteria rather than on
preference, and the reasoning is below so it can be overturned cheaply.

| Characteristic | Fit | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| High ACV | Strong | Six and seven figure enterprise security contracts are routine |
| Spends on events | Strong | RSA and Black Hat are category fixtures with large committed spend |
| Unreachable cold | **Strongest available** | CISOs reportedly field around 60 cold approaches a week and reject most within seconds. Grade C |
| Warm against cold | Strong | Security buys on peer reference. It is the market's own doctrine |
| Converts badly | Strong | Practitioner view that big-booth return "is just not there," while targeted programmes before, during and after an event do produce pipeline. Grade C, opinion |

That last line is a practitioner describing this product without knowing it
exists, which is the closest thing to demand evidence available before a first
sale.

### The alternate, and why it is recorded rather than chosen

**Healthcare and life sciences technology selling into health systems.** High
ACV, conference-gated buying, relationship-driven, and slightly weaker than
cybersecurity on unreachability.

It has one thing cybersecurity does not: **the founder ran the exhibition
programme at a health technology company.** That is operating history in the
segment, and for a business with no delivered engagement, operating history is
scarce.

The two point in different directions. Cybersecurity wins on the stated
criteria, highest cost of problem and highest unreachability. Healthcare wins
on what the founder can already stand behind in a room. Cybersecurity is
selected because the criteria were the instruction. **If the first ten
conversations in security go badly on credibility rather than on fit, that is
the signal to switch, and the switch costs only the vertical wording.**

## The result the adaptation said to watch for

`SKILL-ADAPTATION.md` said to watch for the ICP definition drifting toward who
is easy to reach rather than who has the expensive problem. It drifted the
other way this time and had to be corrected: the previous ICP was defined by a
city, which is a convenience for the operator and means nothing to the buyer.

The corrected definition contains no operator convenience at all. Every one of
the five characteristics is about the client's economics.

## Qualifying and disqualifying

### Qualifies

- Named target account list already exists. Without one there is nothing to
  compose from and no read to run
- A person owns events or field marketing and is measured on pipeline
- Deal cycles long enough that a first conversation matters, which follows from
  characteristic 1
- Has run at least one event that disappointed them, and can say why

### Disqualifies

| Disqualifier | Why |
|---|---|
| ACV below six figures | The arithmetic does not work at any fee that supports the delivery |
| No named account list | Nothing to compose from. Say so on the first call |
| Wants a booth built, a venue found or a production crew | That is an events agency. We are not one |
| Needs a guaranteed count of meetings or leads to report upward | We are not paid on it and will not promise it. Buy an agency that will |
| Believes the channel is unproven and wants convincing of events as a concept | Characteristic 4 fails. The education cost exceeds the deal |

### The one that is tempting and should still be refused

A vendor with high ACV and real budget who wants **one event, run once, to see
if it works.** It fails no stated disqualifier and it will be offered money.

It should still be refused or repriced, because the channel's output is
produced by the post-event conversion step running for weeks, and a single
event bought as a test gets judged on the day. Selling a channel and being
measured on an afternoon is how this business gets a bad first reference.

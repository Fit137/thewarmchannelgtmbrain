## The proof rule for this phase

`SKILL-ADAPTATION.md` is blunt about it and the rule is repeated here because this is the file where it gets broken.

The nine copy drills each ask for a Headline (Social Proof) and a Supporting Proof Point. That is eighteen fields asking for evidence, from a business with no client, no delivered engagement, no testimonial, no named reference and no outcome.

**Every Supporting Proof Point below is `{TBD}`, in all nine drills.** Each one records the specific artifact that would fill it, so the field becomes fillable rather than staying vague.

**Every Headline (Social Proof) slot is substituted with a method or process line,** which is the permitted substitution: describing what the approach is and what it demands of the buyer signals seriousness without claiming a result. None of these lines claims that anyone has bought, attended or endorsed anything.

**The founder's prior record does not appear anywhere in this file.** It is a credential, not a proof point, `GTM-BRIEF.md` Section 5 carries its permitted wording, and four of those claims are gated on an artifact or a permission that does not exist. A credential in permitted wording still does not go in a proof field.

**Two category benchmarks are cited**, both with their source named in the copy. They describe the category, not The Warm Channel, and the copy says so.

| Element | Definition |
|---|---|

| Positioning | Composition and attendance, and nothing else. No venue, no catering, no production, no meeting count and no attendee data, so the host position and the relationships stay with the client. One promise, attendance, with a floor and a remedy attached to it |

**Competitive note, and it constrains this drill.** Pay-on-attendance is not unique. The Ortus Club is reported by clients as charging only for attendees who show up, and MyOutreach states no-shows are never charged. This is a norm among the better operators, not a differentiator. Drill 4 may be used to separate The Warm Channel from trade shows, hosted-buyer operators and in-housing. **It must not be used against slot 1 agencies, because it will be matched in the room.** The differentiator against slot 1 is drill 5, the floor and the remedy.

**Context the seller needs in the room.** This drill argues against a real and specific competitor behaviour. ConvergeX is reported as guaranteeing that at least 50% of attendees book a post-event meeting. A hosted-buyer operator binds delegates contractually to a meeting count with a penalty for early departure, per GDS Group published terms. The buyer is not imagining the promise. They have been quoted one. The refusal has to be argued as a position, not delivered as a shrug.

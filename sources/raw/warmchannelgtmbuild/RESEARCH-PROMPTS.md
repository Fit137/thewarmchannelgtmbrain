# External research prompts, pre-filled

> **HISTORICAL RECORD.** This documents a run completed before the
> 2026-09-22 repositioning in `POSITIONING.md`. It is deliberately not edited:
> a record of what was researched and decided is only useful if it is not
> rewritten afterwards. Read it as evidence of what was done, not as a current
> specification.

Three steps in the skill need a person rather than the model: step 1.1, step 2.1 and step 2.7. Each is filled with The Warm Channel's details and ready to paste into a research tool or a browser.

There is also a step 0 below that the skill does not have. You need it, because the skill assumes you already know your five or six competitors by name, and the brief deliberately names categories instead.

Run step 0 first. Everything after it depends on the slate it produces.

**These four prompts are written for Run A, the paying client.** Run B deltas are at the end rather than duplicated in full.

---

## Step 0. Source the competitor slate

Not in the skill. Do this before Phase 1.

```
I am researching the competitive set for a new business called The Warm Channel.

The Warm Channel is a done-for-you convening service. A client, typically a
fintech or financial infrastructure vendor selling into banks, credit unions
and insurers, gives it a list of named target accounts. The Warm Channel
designs the event theme, builds and verifies the invitation list, runs the
cold outreach, manages confirmations and show rate, and is paid on the
executives who attend. It does not supply the venue, the catering or the
production. The client hosts. First market is the Greater Toronto Area.

Find me real, currently operating examples in each of these categories. For
each, give the name, the website URL, what they charge if that is public, and
who they serve.

1. Agencies that recruit attendees for someone else's executive dinner or
   roundtable, where filling the room is the service being sold.
2. Software and tooling a marketing team buys to do this themselves in-house,
   including event management, account intelligence and invitation platforms.
3. What a vendor selling into banks does today instead: which trade shows and
   industry conferences they exhibit at, and what a booth costs all in.
4. Hosted-buyer and membership summit operators who sell sponsors access to an
   audience the operator owns, rather than to the sponsor's own account list.
5. The in-house hire. Search current job postings for field marketing manager
   and event marketing manager roles at fintech and financial technology
   companies in Canada and the United States. Report the compensation bands,
   the required experience, and the responsibilities listed.
6. Industry associations and trade bodies serving Canadian banking, payments,
   credit union and insurance executives, which may be partners rather than
   rivals.

Tell me for each whether it is Canada-specific or global, and prioritise
Canada-specific and Toronto-specific ones.

Do not include anything you cannot verify with a working URL. For category 5
there is no company, so return the postings themselves with their URLs.
```

**Selection rule when the results come back.** Keep the five or six the buyer would actually consider, not the five or six that look most like The Warm Channel. Expect this reordering: category 4 is what a prospect will name out loud, because those operators are the best known, and category 2 is what they will actually do, because doing it themselves with software they already own is free at the point of decision. If the research comes back with six agencies and no in-house option, it has been run on the wrong question and slot 2 and slot 5 need rerunning on their own.

**Record what you find** in a table with these columns: name, URL, category slot, price if public, who it serves, Canada or global. Phase 2 reads from it.

## Step 1.1. Service demand research

The skill's version of this step researches software features from a features page and a pricing page. This is the adapted version, because these are services and most of the set does not publish either.

```
I need to conduct service demand research for The Warm Channel in the category
of done-for-you executive event convening and attendee recruitment, focused on
fintech and financial infrastructure vendors selling into banks, credit unions
and insurers.

RESEARCH SCOPE

For each competitor below, gather:
- What is actually included in the offer, taken from the sales page, the
  service page and any published scope of work
- How they describe who fills the room, and whether attendee recruitment is
  included or assumed to be the client's job
- Whether they guarantee anything, what exactly is guaranteed, and how the
  guarantee is hedged. Attendance guarantees, meeting guarantees and
  appointment guarantees are three different promises and should be recorded
  separately
- What buyers praise most
- What buyers complain about, or say they wish had been included
- What they promise explicitly, and what they explicitly refuse to promise
- Who owns the attendee list and the attendee data after the event, if stated

COMPETITORS TO RESEARCH:
1. [name] - [url]
2. [name] - [url]
3. [name] - [url]
4. [name] - [url]
5. [name] - [url]
6. [name] - [url]

OUR SERVICE ITEMS TO VALIDATE:
1. The List, a named verified ranked invitation universe for one city and one
   buyer title, sold on its own
2. The Room, one composed room of 12 to 20 seats built from the client's own
   named account list
3. The Channel, four rooms across twelve months from a standing invitation
   universe
4. In-person facilitation on the day
5. Written summary and media capture
6. Composition report sold standalone to a client running its own room
7. Consented introductions after the room

For each of ours, tell me: does anyone in the competitor set already offer
this, how central is it to their offer, and is there public evidence that
buyers want it.

SOURCES TO CHECK: the competitors' own sites and case studies, read for what
they avoid claiming as much as what they claim. LinkedIn posts by named
clients and by the operators themselves. Conference talks and panel
appearances. Trade press covering B2B field marketing and event marketing.
Job postings, for the in-house alternative.

Note that this category is not reviewed on G2 or Capterra. Those are software
review sites. Do not substitute a software company that happens to match a
name.

Return findings in a structured format.
```

## Step 2.1. Competitor deep research

```
I need to conduct competitive research for the done-for-you executive event
convening and attendee recruitment category, with focus on fintech and
financial infrastructure vendors selling into banks, credit unions and
insurers in Canada.

RESEARCH SCOPE

For each competitor below, gather:
- Website URL
- Tagline from the homepage
- Sub-tagline or secondary headline
- Full description from the homepage and about page
- Strengths, from reviews, testimonials and public discussion
- Weaknesses, from complaints and negative feedback
- Who they say they serve, in their own words
- What they charge, if public
- Whether they sell access to an audience they own, or composition of the
  client's own list. This distinction is the positioning axis for the whole
  category and it is the first thing to record

COMPETITORS TO RESEARCH:
1. [name] - [url]
2. [name] - [url]
3. [name] - [url]
4. [name] - [url]
5. [name] - [url]
6. The Warm Channel (ours) - no site yet

SOURCES TO CHECK: competitor sites and case studies, LinkedIn, trade press on
B2B events and field marketing, published terms and conditions where they
exist, and job postings for the in-house alternative.

Note that this category is not reviewed on G2 or Capterra. Those are software
review sites, and pointing this research at them will return an unrelated
software company. Do not let one enter the analysis.

Return findings in a structured format matching the attributes above.
```

**Note on our own row.** The skill asks you to research your own product alongside the competitors. There is no site and no reviews, so fill that row from Section 1 of `GTM-BRIEF.md` rather than from a crawl, and mark strengths and weaknesses `{TBD}` rather than writing aspirational ones. A self-assessment written as evidence is the fastest way to get a wrong Phase 2, and it is the row most likely to be quoted back three phases later as though it had been researched.

## Step 2.7. Review analysis, substituted

The skill sends you to G2 for a four and five star against one to three star breakdown. This category is not on G2. Same question, different sources.

```
I need review analysis to define positioning vectors for the done-for-you
executive event convening category.

RESEARCH SCOPE

For each competitor below, find published buyer feedback and analyse it.

WHAT BUYERS LOVE:
- Extract repeated positive themes
- Identify what each one is seen as leading in
- Count how often each theme appears

WHAT BUYERS DISLIKE OR WISH FOR:
- Extract repeated complaints
- Identify gaps and unmet wishes
- Count how often each theme appears

COMPETITORS TO ANALYSE:
1. [name] - [url]
2. [name] - [url]
3. [name] - [url]
4. [name] - [url]
5. [name] - [url]

WHERE TO LOOK: LinkedIn posts and comments from people who attended or paid
for these events, including posts from the attendee side rather than only the
sponsor side. Reddit and industry forums for sales and marketing leaders.
Trade press. Published case studies read for what they omit. Conference talks
where a field marketer describes what worked and what did not.

Then, separately from the competitor analysis, research the attendee side of
this category:
- Are senior executives in banking, payments, risk and technology reporting
  fatigue with vendor-hosted dinners and roundtables, and in what terms
- What do they say makes them accept an invitation, and what makes them
  decline
- Is there evidence of a shift toward peer-only formats that exclude vendors,
  and where is it strongest by sector

OUTPUT NEEDED:
For each competitor: top 3 positive themes with frequency, top 3 negative
themes with frequency. Then, for the attendee research, the stated reasons for
accepting and declining, ranked by how often they appear.

Return findings in a structured format.
```

**What to watch for.** Two findings are likely and both matter downstream. First, the complaints about this category are expected to cluster on attendance rather than on production: the room was set for twenty and eight came, or the people who came were not the people promised. That is the opening, and it lands in Phase 6 as the pillar and in Phase 9 as the reason the attendance floor is the value metric. Second, the attendee-side research is likely to show that executives are actively avoiding vendor dinners in the most saturated sectors. That is a finding about Run B, it is the reason the brief picked fintech over cybersecurity, and if the research contradicts it the niche choice in Section 1 needs revisiting before Phase 3 rather than after.

## Run B deltas, the guest side

Do not duplicate the four prompts. Four changes.

**Category and niche.** The category is not a market, because the guest is not buying. Research the question of what makes a named bank, credit union or insurer executive in Canada accept a private invitation from someone they do not know.

**The slate.** The competitive set is whatever else is competing for the same evening: a rival vendor's dinner, an industry association event, a peer-only network, a webinar that costs nothing to skip, and the guest's own calendar. Source it from what these executives publicly attend and post about, not from a vendor directory.

**The sources.** LinkedIn activity of the target titles, association member lists and event pages, published speaker lists from Canadian financial services events, and any public commentary from these executives on events they attended.

**The extra question, and it is the one that decides the business.** Under Canadian anti-spam law and privacy law, what is the defensible basis for a first unsolicited approach to a named executive at a Canadian financial institution, what does implied consent from a published business contact detail actually cover, and what does it not cover. Get a written answer from counsel rather than from a research tool. This is open decision 5 in the brief and it gates the entire Run B channel set.

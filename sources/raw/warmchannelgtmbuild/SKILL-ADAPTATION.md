# Running gtm-builder on The Warm Channel

The skill is `B2B SaaS Go-To-Market Builder`. The Warm Channel is a done-for-you service sold as an engagement, with two audiences and only one of them paying, so six of the nine phases carry an assumption that produces the wrong artifact, and the whole sequence runs twice.

Read this before Phase 1. Without it the run produces four subscription tiers for a business that sells engagements, a G2 review analysis for a category that is not on G2, and a value proposition phase that asks eighteen times for proof from a business that has none.

| Phase | Verdict |
|---|---|
| 1. Product Feature Matrix | Six-vector service matrix |
| 2. Competitive Market Analysis | Substitute sources, add in-housing |
| 3. Positioning | As written, twice |
| 4. ICP | Maturity reading |
| 5. Buying Committee | As written for Run A, collapses for Run B |
| 6. Value Proposition | As written, fabrication risk is high |
| 7. Customer Journey Funnel | As written, add the payment blocker |
| 8. Assets and Collaterals | Replace all three channels |
| 9. Pricing and Packaging | Engagement pricing, Run A only |
| Lead Magnet Scorecard | As written |
| 10-Slide Deck | As written, with the claim ledger open beside it |

Phases 3 and 6, the lead magnet and the deck are universal, so that is where the run can move fastest. Run B can borrow the structure of Phases 3, 6 and 7 from Run A, and none of the copy.

---

## Phase 1. Product Feature Matrix

**Assumes:** discrete software features scored on two axes, demand and competitiveness.

**Why it breaks:** the seven items in Section 3 of the brief are services with wildly different delivery loads. In-person facilitation and consented introductions will both score high on demand and high on differentiation, and the two-axis version will tell you to keep them. Both consume founder hours that do not scale, and one of them is a pipeline promise in disguise.

**Instead:** rate each service on six vectors. Client Demand, Competitive Differentiation, Delivery Effort, Sales Motion, Ticket Size and Funnel Role. Plot Demand against Differentiation exactly as steps 1.3 to 1.5 describe, and carry vectors 3 to 6 as a second table read alongside the scatter. Steps 1.6 and 1.7, the demand driver, run unchanged and feed both the lead magnet and the Phase 8 top of funnel asset.

**The result to watch for.** The List should come out as the highest-value item in the set despite the smallest ticket, because its funnel role is qualification and its delivery effort is low, and because it is the only item that builds guest-side inventory while the client pays for it. If the matrix does not surface that, the Funnel Role vector has been scored as an afterthought.

---

## Phase 2. Competitive Market Analysis

**Assumes:** the competitors are reviewed on G2, Capterra and Trustpilot, and step 2.7 does a four and five star against one to three star breakdown.

**Why it breaks:** those are software review sites. None of the six slots in the brief is a software product, two of them are not companies at all, and a search there will return an unrelated software company with a similar name that then enters the analysis as a competitor.

**Instead:** keep step 2.7's question, which is the valuable part, and change where it is asked. For agencies and summit operators, read published case studies for what they carefully avoid claiming, then look at posts from named clients and at conference talks. Expect thin evidence and record it as thin rather than filling the gap.

Two slots need a different method entirely. Slot 2, in-housing, and slot 5, the hire, have no reviews. Profile both from job postings: read the compensation band, the required experience and the time to hire as the cost, and read the responsibilities list as the scope the buyer currently believes they need.

**The own-product row.** The skill asks you to research your own product alongside the competitors. There is nothing to crawl. Fill that row from Section 1 of the brief and mark strengths and weaknesses `{TBD}` rather than writing aspirational ones. A self-assessment written as evidence is the fastest way to get a wrong Phase 2.

## Phase 4. ICP

**Assumes:** ICP Level 1 is MVP to PMF and Level 2 is Growth. Those are stages of a software company, and this buyer is a marketing function inside a bank's vendor.

**Instead:** read the levels as how far the buyer has got with the problem. Level 1 is a vendor that has never run its own executive room. Level 2 is a vendor that has run one, got eight people in a room set for twenty, and wants it done properly.

**The result to watch for.** Level 2 should come out as the better buyer, which inverts the usual reading. A buyer who has already failed at this knows what the failure costs, has a number for it, and does not need the category explained. Level 1 has to be sold the format before being sold the vendor, which is two sales.

---

## Phase 5. Buying Committee

**Runs as written for Run A.** User is the field marketing or demand generation lead. Manager is the VP Marketing. Decision Maker is the CMO, with the CRO involved where the budget moves across from sales.

**Fill the Manager's Potential Reasons to Block row carefully.** The person whose job partly is this work is the person most likely to read the purchase as a judgment on their performance, and a purchase made over their head dies quietly in scheduling no matter how good it is. The answer is to sell the capacity argument rather than the competence argument, and Phase 6 needs to know that.

**Collapses for Run B.** The guest is user, manager and decision maker in one person. Do not invent three personas to fill the table. Fill one column honestly, note the collapse, and move the effort to step 5.2.

**Step 5.2 is the most valuable step in the run for Run B.** The Design Partner profile defines the founding guest cohort, and a founding guest cohort is the entire supply side of this business. Treat it as a deliverable in its own right, not an appendix. Twenty named executives who will accept a first invitation is worth more than everything Run A produces, because Run A cannot be delivered without them.

---

## Phase 6, the fabrication risk

Include this whatever else changes. It is a property of the skill rather than of the business.

Phase 6 asks for a Supporting Proof Point and a Headline (Social Proof) in each of nine copy drills. Eighteen fields asking for evidence, from a business that has none.

**What The Warm Channel has: nothing.** No client, no delivered engagement, no testimonial, no named reference, no outcome. Fill both fields with `{TBD}` in all nine drills. Method and process are legitimate substitutes for the social proof headline, because describing what the approach is and what it demands of the buyer signals seriousness without claiming a result. The attendance floor and its remedy are the strongest honest material available for these fields, because a stated consequence is evidence of confidence in a way that an adjective is not.

**The specific trap here.** The founder does have a real record, in another business and a prior role. Phase 6 will reach for it, and Section 5 of the brief has the permitted wording for every piece of it. Three of those claims are currently gated on artifacts that do not exist yet and one is gated on a permission that has not been requested. A credential in permitted wording is not a proof point, and it does not go in the proof fields.

This is where a fabricated testimonial enters a run that was otherwise honest, because the template asks nine times and templates get filled.

---

## Phase 7. Customer Journey Funnel

**Runs as written,** with two changes.

**Stage 7, Renewal and Loyalty,** assumes a recurring contract. For Run A it reads as the follow-on engagement, which is the second room and then the annual programme. For Run B it reads as the guest accepting a second invitation, which is the only measure of whether the room was any good.

**Add the payment blocker to Primary Journey Blockers, and put it first.** There is no registered business name and no tax number, so there is no invoice that a corporate accounts payable department will accept. That is an infrastructure problem that surfaces as a funnel problem at the last stage, after every earlier stage has worked. It is open decision 1 in the brief.

---

## Phase 8. Assets and Collaterals

**Assumes three campaigns, hardcoded:** LinkedIn outreach via HeyReach, cold email via Instantly, and LinkedIn ads.

**Why it breaks here in a way it does not break elsewhere.** This business argues that cold digital outreach is decaying and expensive. Running high-volume cold email as its own primary channel is both a weak bet on the numbers and a credibility problem the moment a prospect notices. Paid ads against an unproven offer with no conversion data buys expensive evidence, which a pre-launch business cannot afford. And Canadian anti-spam law is the operating environment for the core activity, not a footnote, which is open decision 5.

**Keep the three by three grid and replace all three channels:**

| Channel | Why |
|---|---|
| Founder-led organic and low-volume, high-research direct approach | The thesis and the channel agree. Small numbers of well-researched approaches is also the method being sold, so the acquisition motion demonstrates the product |
| A demonstration room, run at the founder's own cost, with prospective clients invited to observe | The product as its own top of funnel asset. It creates the first reference, the first guest cohort and the first auditable funnel table in one exercise, and it converts the pre-launch problem into an asset rather than waiting it out |
| Partnerships with the slot 6 institutions and with service providers already selling to fintech vendors | Borrowed credibility while there is none of its own, and a second route to guest-side supply |

Name the reasoning rather than just the substitution, because whoever runs Phase 8 may disagree and should be able to argue with it.

**For Run B the three are different again:** nomination from confirmed guests, founder direct approach, and institutional partnership. No advertising, no sequenced email.

---

## Phase 9. Pricing and Packaging

**Assumes a subscription.** Value metric denominators of per user, per seat, per usage, per outcome and per capability, and a Decoy, Hero, Anchor structure across four plans.

**Why it breaks:** this is sold as an engagement. Four subscription plans do not describe it, and the per-outcome denominator is the one option that must not be selected, because pricing this on the client's pipeline is an outcome claim with a payment schedule attached.

**Instead:** build engagement pricing. Scope, duration, what is included, what is passed through, and what a second engagement costs. The value metric is the attending in-ICP executive, which is a real per-unit denominator and makes the three scopes in Section 3 comparable to each other.

**Every number stays `{TBD}`.** The bands in the strategy conclusion of 2026-09-16 are an analyst proposal, not a founder decision, and open decision 2 has not been settled. The phase still runs and is still worth running, because the tier shape, the value metric and the ROI structure are worth having before the number exists.

**Step 9.3, ROI anchoring, is the strongest artifact in the phase and it is universal.** Keep it. Anchor 2, the alternative comparison, is the one that closes here, because two of the six competitor slots are things the buyer already pays for and can price: the all-in cost of a trade show booth, and the loaded cost of the field marketing hire. Both are in the brief's cleared figures table with their grades. Do not use the grade C figures for this.

**Run B has no pricing phase.** Substitute an attendance cost reading: what the guest gives up, what they get, and what makes the second acceptance more likely than the first. That is the phase that decides whether guest-side inventory compounds or has to be rebuilt every time.

## Time

The skill estimates 5 to 6 hours across multiple sessions, one phase per session with review between. This is two runs, so budget 8 to 10 hours rather than 12, because Run B borrows the structure of Phases 3, 6 and 7 from Run A and has no Phase 9.

Three steps need a person rather than the model: step 1.1, step 2.1 and step 2.7. `RESEARCH-PROMPTS.md` has all three pre-filled, plus the step 0 they depend on. Run step 0 first, because the slate in the brief names categories rather than confirmed competitors.

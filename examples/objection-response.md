# Example, written reply to a guarantee objection

Status: DRAFT, pending founder approval. Its stance on guarantees holds until D4 and D10 land, then it is rewritten.
Audience: client prospect, a VP Marketing, the Manager. Stage: evaluation. Source: `reference/objection-handling.md`, `reference/competitor-battlecards.md`.

The prospect wrote: "Another agency we spoke to guarantees half the attendees book a follow-up meeting. Can you match that?"

---

Hi [first name],

Straight answer: no, I will not guarantee a number of meetings, and I would rather tell you why now than have it surface in a contract.

A guaranteed meeting count gets priced into the fee, which means you pay for it whether or not the meetings are worth having. If a meeting number is what you have to report upward this quarter, that agency is a reasonable choice, and I would say so to your face.

What I can show you before you spend anything is which of your named accounts could actually be in the room, and what it would take to get them there. That is the coverage read, and it is free.

If the read says your list cannot support it, I will tell you that too.

[Founder's name]

---

## Why this works

- **It answers the question in the first sentence.** No hedging, no pivot.
- **It argues the refusal as a position**, not a shrug: a guarantee is priced into the fee.
- **It leads with the competitor's genuine strength**, reportability, and concedes the fit honestly. That is what makes it credible.
- **It never says the competitor's guarantee is false.** We have no evidence either way.
- **It offers the one thing we can deliver today**, the coverage read, and promises an honest bad answer, which is evidence of confidence.
- **No number, no client, no result.**

## Watch for
- If D4 sets a promise and remedy, the third paragraph gains it, in contract language.
- If D10 prices per qualified lead, the second paragraph changes. Rewrite rather than patch.

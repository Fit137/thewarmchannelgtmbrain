# Example, founder LinkedIn post, category archetype

Status: DRAFT, produced 2026-10-07 as the Phase 9 live test, using only this brain. Pending founder approval.
Audience: client. Stage: awareness. Template: `templates/linkedin-post.md`. Figures: `rules/evidence-ledger.md` rows 11 and 12.

---

Vendor-owned conferences get 19% of registrants through the door. Invite-only executive dinners get 70%.

Both free to attend. Both aimed at senior B2B buyers. Clutch Events counted 13,155 registrations across 191 invite-only, free-to-attend executive B2B events in 2025 and early 2026, and that is the gap they found.

It is not the catering.

A conference registration costs the buyer nothing and asks nothing of them. An invitation to a small table, from someone who chose them by name, on a question they are actually dealing with, is a different request. People turn up for the second kind.

Here is the arithmetic. At 70%, twelve people in the room means about seventeen confirmations. Seventeen named senior people saying yes, from your own account list, is weeks of individual work. That arithmetic is the job.

If you want to know how many people on your account list could actually be in that room, send me the list and I will send back a coverage read.

---

## Why this works

- **The hook is a dated, sourced contrast**, not an adjective. Two numbers from one benchmark, attributed in the second line with its sample size.
- **It describes the category, never us.** Nothing claims The Warm Channel achieves 70%. The figure is labelled as what Clutch Events found.
- **The turn is three words**, "It is not the catering", then the reframe about what the request asks of the buyer.
- **Capacity, never competence.** "That arithmetic is the job" puts the difficulty in the numbers, not in the reader's team.
- **One soft call to action**, the coverage read, which needs no track record.
- **A guest reading it does not feel like inventory.** It talks about why people accept a good invitation, not about how to extract them.

# Channel engine, how The Warm Channel acquires clients and guests

Load for: any founder post, direct approach, demonstration event or partnership asset. Synthesized from Run A Phase 8, Run B Phase 8 and the evidence ledger. Every channel template points back here.

## Thesis

**The acquisition motion is the product, demonstrated.** The Warm Channel argues that volume channels are decaying and that small numbers of well-researched approaches to people who matter outperform them. So it acquires its own clients and guests exactly that way. If it ran sequences at volume, the argument would be false in the seller's own hands and a prospect would notice. If low-volume research works, the motion becomes the only case study available before the first engagement.

## How the channels feed each other

```
Founder posts (teardown, category, arithmetic)
      |  attention from in-ICP marketers
      v
Coverage read offered  <---  Direct approach, one researched message
      |  a number about their own list
      v
Book a call  --->  scope (BLOCKED D1, D2, D4)  --->  engagement
                                                       |
Partnerships ---> standing + guest supply ----------->  |
                                                       v
Demonstration event ---> observers (client side) + cohort (guest side)
                    ---> first funnel table + first reference ---> proof set
```

## Client side, three channels

1. **Founder-led organic and low-volume direct approach.** READY. Posts, plus one researched approach per prospect, sender identified, no sequence. Client-side prospecting is added to the D5 re-scope so counsel confirms it.
2. **The demonstration event.** One event at the founder's own cost, on a question live in the chosen segment, prospective clients invited to observe and disclosed to guests. BLOCKED, D5 and runway. The single highest-value action in the plan.
3. **Partnerships.** Associations and service providers already selling to the segment. Borrowed credibility and guest supply. Partner set re-sourced after D8, D15. List ownership written before the first conversation.

## Guest side, three routes

Nomination from confirmed guests (after a first event), founder direct approach (BLOCKED, D5), institutional partnership (open today). No advertising, no sequences, no follow-up to a non-reply, ever.

## Post archetypes, rotate them

| Archetype | What it does | Figures allowed |
|---|---|---|
| **Composition teardown** | A named public event read for who was there, from public sources, against who the host presumably wanted. The demand driver in public | None needed. Public sources only, no speculation about named individuals, no claim about the host's results |
| **Category post** | Vendor-owned conferences attend at 19%, invite-only executive dinners at 70%. What separates them, and why it is not the catering | Clutch Events, with sample size, said as a category benchmark |
| **The arithmetic** | Twelve seats needs about seventeen confirmations. Why the gap between invited and arrived is the whole job | Clutch Events 70% |
| **The budget wedge** | 40.8% of exhibitor budgets go to the booth. The same money, run as a channel | CEIR 2026 |
| **The reply-rate trend** | 8.5% in 2019 to 3.43% in 2026, stated with dates, and stop | Instantly |
| **The scope post** | What we do not supply and why it protects the client: no venue, no catering, no production, no rented audience | None |

## Plan, write, edit

- **Plan.** One audience (client), one stage, one archetype, one takeaway in a sentence. If the takeaway will not fit in a sentence, stop.
- **Write.** Hook in the first line, the specific detail by the third, the turn or reframe, then one soft call to action or a question, never both. 300 to 500 words for a teardown, shorter for the rest.
- **Edit.** Cut the warm-up. Check every figure against the evidence ledger and every capability against the status ledger. Check that a guest reading it would not feel like inventory. Run the QA checklist.

## Hook patterns

A number with its date ("Cold email reply rates were 8.5% in 2019."). A named public event ("Who was actually in the room at last week's summit?"). The arithmetic ("Twelve seats. Seventeen confirmations."). The reframe ("The money is already being spent.").

## The direct approach

One named person, researched properly, approached once, with the reason in the first line. Short. Names the host where there is one. **Once.** No sequence, no follow-up, no second attempt on the same question. A non-reply is an answer. Twenty well-researched approaches beat four hundred sent.

## Calls to action

Website: "Book a call", only. Posts: one soft line, usually the coverage read: "Send one target account list and get the coverage read back." Guest messages: "Reply yes or no. Either is a fine answer."

## What good looks like, measured

Not posting frequency. Coverage reads requested by in-ICP titles. Reads that reach a call. Calls that reach scope. On the guest side: acceptances, arrivals against confirmations, repeat acceptance, nominations per guest. Definitions in `ops/kpi-dictionary.md`.

## Excluded, and the engine refuses them

Paid advertising on any platform. Sequenced cold email. HeyReach-style or any automated LinkedIn outreach. Purchased lists and scraped personal addresses. A content calendar built on posting frequency. Advertised guest invitations, which select for people with free evenings.

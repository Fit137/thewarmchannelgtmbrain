# Value proposition, guest

Load for: any invitation, reconfirmation, note afterwards, summary or partner-routed message. Distils Run B Phases 3, 6 and 7. Structure borrowed from the client side, copy borrowed from nothing. Every line in the copy sections is guest vocabulary. Check `rules/two-vocabularies.md` before adding any.

## The competitor is the guest's own evening

The guest is not buying. The competition is everything else competing for the same evening: another vendor's dinner, an association event, a peer-only network, a webinar, and above all **their own calendar and their family, which wins most evenings and should be assumed to.** Read every guest decision against going home instead.

Vendor-dinner fatigue is documented among CISOs (68% say aggressive marketing hides real innovation, grade C plus). For other functions it is untested. Assume the fatigue is present until someone asks.

## Promise and positioning

| Element | Wording | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Brand promise | "An evening with a small number of people dealing with the same problem you are, on a question set before anyone was invited." | Approved |
| Positioning | "A closed room, no stage time, no pitching from the floor, no list circulated." | Approved. The original continued "and no follow-up sequence... nothing asked afterwards", frozen by D11 |

## Pillars

| Pillar | RTB 1 | RTB 2 | RTB 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The question is real** | The question is set before anyone is invited, and named in the first line | It comes from what is live at organisations like theirs, not from what the host sells | Nobody presents. No deck, no panel |
| **The room is the point** | Who is coming is described by role and organisation type before anyone accepts | Small enough that everyone speaks and is expected to | No other vendors or consultants. The host is named and is not selling from the table |
| **What happens afterwards** | No attendee list is circulated, before or after | FROZEN, D11: "No contact details go to anyone, including the host" | FROZEN, D11: "No follow-up sequence. A written summary if you want one, and otherwise silence" |

The third pillar won the second acceptance and was the cheapest to keep. Post-event conversion, the client-side hero, contradicts two of its three lines. D11 decides which side moves. The recommended route is consented continuation: the guest chooses, at or after the evening, whether to continue a conversation with the host, and nothing passes without that choice.

Note on wording, a PATCH for founder confirmation: the source said "institution" because the first guests were bankers. "Organisation" is the segment-neutral form until D14, and the practitioners-only line gained "like yours" so it still means buyer-side, not vendor-side. "Twelve seats. Everyone speaks" describes one specific evening and may be used when that evening has twelve seats.

## Frozen lines, D11

"Your details are not passed to the host or to anyone else." "You will not be added to anything." "No follow-up sequence, no calls, no next steps." "No one will chase you afterwards." "Say yes to one evening, not to a relationship." "One evening, a known start and a known end, and nothing asked of you afterwards." Keep them out of every live message until D11 lands.

## Three real surfaces

| Surface | Draws on |
|---|---|
| The invitation | The question first, where it came from, who else by role, the size, and "no attendee list goes out" |
| The reconfirmation, close to the date | Nobody presents, practitioners only, the start and end time, any material change to the room |
| The note afterwards | The summary offer, one nomination ask ("who should have been in that room"), and whatever D11 settles |

## Proof

No cohort exists, so social proof is `{TBD}` everywhere. Who else is in the room is the guest's proof, and once a cohort exists it becomes the strongest guest-facing asset the business will ever have. A proof point aimed at a guest is a category error: they are deciding about an evening, not evaluating a supplier.

## The competence trap, and the only way through

The person who most needs this bought is the person it implicitly criticises. A purchase made over their head is not refused, it is slow-walked: the list arrives late, the date moves twice, and the engagement dies in scheduling with nobody having said no.

**Sell capacity, never competence.** The failure is arithmetic, not skill. Getting named senior people to commit takes sustained outreach against people who do not reply, over weeks, and no marketing generalist running six other channels has those weeks. At the Clutch benchmark of 70% attendance for invite-only executive dinners, twelve people in the room needs about seventeen confirmations, and seventeen confirmations from named senior people is the work. Nobody underperformed. The arithmetic was never done.

No pillar, headline or line of copy may imply the buyer's team did this badly.

## Design partner, the first paying clients

| Attribute | Specification |
|---|---|
| Profile | Meets all five ICP characteristics. Ran an owned event in the last two quarters that disappointed. Has a named account list. Will host. Willing to be first and knows it |
| Titles | Field Marketing Manager, Demand Generation Manager, Director of Field Marketing, in the D8 segment |
| Pain | Cannot get named executives to accept. Outbound reply rates falling. Trade show spend producing scans. A specific recent event with a specific cost |
| Triggers | Planning the next event now. Budget cycle. A field marketing hire not approved or not working. A competitor seen hosting |
| Decision criteria | Will the right people be there. What exactly is promised and what happens if it is missed. Do we keep the relationship. Can I explain this to my VP without it sounding like an admission |
| Barriers | No tax number on the invoice, D1. No references. Solo supplier. The competence reading. Vendor onboarding |
| Success metrics | Named-account coverage. Arrivals against the promise once D4 sets it. Whether the VP funded a second engagement |
| Early adopter tell | Has been burned by a promised outcome and says so unprompted. Prefers a small promise kept to a large one hedged. Comfortable being a reference later |

**Offer a design partner**: founding client terms, D13, direct founder delivery, a free coverage read, and a written promise once D4 settles. **Never offer**: equity in outcomes, a meeting count, or ownership of the guest list. A design partner who negotiates the list away has taken the only asset the business has.

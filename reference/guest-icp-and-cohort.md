# Guest ICP and the founding guest cohort

Load for: who the guests are, why anchors come first, how the cohort gets built, and what must never be done to it. Distils Run B Phases 1 to 5. Internal file. Its words are not guest copy.

## Why the guest side is not the second half of the client side

The guest side is supply. No client engagement can be delivered without guests, and returning guests, not the method, are the asset that compounds. Being second in sequence does not make it second in importance. Twenty named executives who will accept a first invitation are worth more than every client-side document, because those documents cannot be delivered without them. **Guest inventory starts at zero.** The healthcare guest list is out of scope on consent grounds, D6.

## What the guest is offered, six items

| Item | Wins | Our effort | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| The question, one live problem shared by everyone there | The first acceptance | High, real research | The only reason a senior person gives up an evening |
| The peers, described by role and organisation type | The first acceptance | High, and circular | Each acceptance makes the next easier |
| The format, small, closed, no stage time, no pitching | The second acceptance | Medium, refusals enforced on the night | The claim is everywhere, the practice is not |
| A known time cost, start, end, no obligation | Removes a quiet reason to decline | Low | Hosted-buyer operators do the opposite |
| Discretion, no list circulated, no details passed, no sequence | **The largest determinant of a second acceptance** | Low, it is a policy | Partly frozen by D11. "No list circulated" stays |
| The summary, if they want it | The cheapest reason to accept again | Medium | Attributes nothing to a named person without permission |

## Two levels, read as position in the room

| | Accessible guest | Anchor guest |
|---|---|---|
| Who | Director or senior manager in the functions the client sells to | VP, SVP or C-level, at an organisation the others recognise |
| Why they accept | Building a network, wants the answer, fewer invitations | Rarely. For the question, or for who else is coming |
| Acceptance rate | Higher | Assume low |
| Cost to us | Research and one approach | Sustained work, a warm route, often a nomination |
| Worth | Fills the room. An anchor in two years | **Makes the room.** One anchor converts several accessible acceptances |
| Risk | A room of only accessible guests is one the client will not pay for twice | A room built for one anchor who cancels has nothing holding it |

**Secure anchors first and build around them.** Acceptance runs downhill. An accessible guest is reassured by an anchor. An anchor is not reassured by three directors.

**Re-derive for the segment, D14.** The 2026-09-18 guest ICP was VP and Director level in payments, risk, digital, data, fraud, technology and operations at Canadian banks, credit unions and insurers. Under the cybersecurity default the equivalent is the security, risk and technology leadership of the enterprises the client sells into. Confirm with D8 before any approach.

## The cohort target

Twenty named executives who will accept a first invitation: four to six anchors, fourteen to sixteen accessible guests, across at least three functions. **Across functions matters most.** A cohort of twenty in one function serves one kind of client. Spread across three, it serves three, and the fourth client's audience is built from the same relationships. Inventory that serves one client is a contract. Inventory that serves several is a business.

Exclude consultants and other vendors. They change what gets said.

## How it gets built, in order

| Route | Method | Yield | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Institutional partnership | An association supplies the question and the standing. The Warm Channel supplies composition | Highest quality, slowest. Cleanest consent, via the member relationship | **Open today.** Settle list ownership before the first conversation |
| 2 Founder direct approach | Named individuals, researched properly, approached once, question in the first line | Low rate, high quality. Also the method sold to clients | BLOCKED, D5 |
| 3 Nomination from a confirmed guest | After an event, ask each guest for one peer who should have been there | Highest conversion of any route | Exists only after a first event |
| 4 The demonstration event | Founder's own cost. First cohort, first reference, first funnel table together | The unblocking move | BLOCKED, D5 and runway |

## Never do this to the cohort

| Never | Because |
|---|---|
| Port the healthcare guest list | A different sender and purpose. A consent breach before a brand error |
| Hand a guest's details to a client without the guest's consent | It ends the cohort. Guests talk, and this group is small enough that all will hear. D11 decides the consented form |
| Circulate the attendee list, before or after | The cheapest promise to keep and the most expensive to break |
| Let a client pitch from the floor | Converts a guest into an audience in one evening |
| Show a guest client-facing copy | They learn they were inventory |
| Claim the cohort to a client before it exists | Hard ban on implying a standing network |
| Spend an anchor on a client whose product they would never buy | It burns the scarcest relationship on an evening that cannot be repeated |
| Invite a good guest again within weeks | Over-inviting is how a cohort gets spent |

**The measure.** Not twenty acceptances. Twenty executives who accepted, arrived, and would accept again.

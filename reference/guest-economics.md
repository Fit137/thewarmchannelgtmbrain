# Guest economics, the attendance cost reading

Load for: deciding whether a guest's side of the trade holds, designing an evening, and protecting the second acceptance. Distils Run B Phase 9, the substitute for a pricing phase. Internal file.

The guest pays nothing, so there is no pricing phase on this side. The equivalent question is whether the guest's trade holds up, because if it does not, there is nothing to sell the client.

## What the guest gives up

| They give | Real cost | Note |
|---|---|---|
| The evening | Three to four hours including travel, on a weeknight | The largest cost and the most understated. State the end time |
| Family time or rest | Unquantifiable and often decisive | The guest's own calendar wins most evenings |
| Reputational exposure | Being seen at a vendor-hosted event, saying something peers will remember | Rises with seniority. Why anchors decline more often, and it is not timidity |
| Attention in advance | Deciding, diarising, remembering | Where most acceptances quietly decay before the date |
| Candour | Saying what they actually think | **The real ask.** An evening where nobody says anything true wastes everyone's time |

## What they get

| They get | Delivered by | Reliability |
|---|---|---|
| A live question answered by peers who have handled it | Question research | The highest-variance input in the business |
| Three or four people worth knowing in their own function | Composition | Good, if the no-vendors rule holds |
| Permission to say something they would not say publicly | The closed format, held | Only as good as the last time it was held |
| A written summary, if wanted | Follow-up | Reliable and cheap |
| No list circulated, and whatever D11 settles about afterwards | Refusals kept | The most reliable thing on offer, because it costs nothing |

## Whether the trade holds

**Accessible guest: yes, comfortably.** An evening for three useful contacts and a live question is a fair trade for a director building a network.

**Anchor guest: marginal on the first invitation, good on the second.** A senior executive has more invitations than evenings. The first acceptance is bought almost entirely by the question and by who is already confirmed. The second is bought by the first evening having been good, which is cheap.

**So effort belongs at the front, in question selection and anchor acceptance, not at scale.** Growing the cohort by approaching more people rather than choosing better questions drives the acceptance rate down as the volume goes up.

## What makes the second acceptance more likely than the first

The compounding mechanism.

| Factor | Effect | Cost | Priority |
|---|---|---|---|
| The refusals were kept: no list, nothing passed without consent, no chasing | **Largest single factor**, the promise most often broken in this category | Zero | 1 |
| The evening ended when it was said it would end | Large, almost never done | Zero | 2 |
| The question was real, and someone there had solved it | Large | High, the scarce skill | 3 |
| The room was what they were told | Large. Overstating it loses a guest permanently | Zero, costs acceptances at stage 3 | 4 |
| Nobody pitched, including the host | Large | Medium. Someone must be in the room, D7 | 5 |
| The summary arrived and was good | Medium | Low | 6 |
| They were asked for one nomination, not a list | Medium, turns a guest into a supply route | Zero | 7 |
| The next invitation came months later, not weeks | Medium. Over-inviting spends a cohort | Zero, and discipline | 8 |

Five of eight cost nothing. The guest side is won mostly by not doing things, which suits a business with `{TBD}` runway that cannot buy its way to a cohort.

**D11 sits on factor 1.** Post-event conversion, as drafted on the landing page, breaks the refusals that matter most. Consented continuation keeps factor 1 intact because nothing passes without the guest's choice.

**D7 sits on factor 5.** The founder attending the first three is how the format survives contact with a paying client.

## The measure

Not attendance. **Repeat acceptance rate and nominations per attending guest.** Both `{TBD}` until an event happens. Start measuring both on day one of the demonstration event. They are worth more than anything the client side measures.

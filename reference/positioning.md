# Positioning

Load for: what category we are in, what we lead on, and which positioning statement to use with whom. Distils `POSITIONING.md` (2026-09-22) and Run A Phase 3 (rebuilt 2026-09-22).

## Category and the one line

**Category**: a done-for-you B2B lead generation channel built on in-person events.
**One line**: a lead generation channel that happens to run in person.
**What we do**: we plan the channel, we fill it with your people, and we work every conversation after it. The last clause is held for publication until D11.

## What it is, and what it is not

| It is | It is not |
|---|---|
| Strategy, execution, the people in the room, and the work afterwards that turns them into sales-qualified leads. All of it | Attendance, which is one step of four and undersells the whole |
| A channel whose output is a sales-qualified lead | Convening, which is a component |
| Format-agnostic: a table of ten, a summit of two hundred, a programme around a show the client already exhibits at | An events agency, which is paid to produce an event, not for the channel's output |
| Deliverable anywhere. The page names no country or city | A venue, catering or production supplier |

**The four components live on the sales call, never on the website.** Putting them on the page turns one outcome into four confusing products and invites the client to buy the cheapest one.

## The dream outcome

Highly qualified B2B sales leads at the moment every other channel is degrading. Not leads, MQLs or meetings. Sales-qualified leads from people who met a human being, in a room, for long enough to form a view. The source wording continued "that no other channel can produce", and that clause fails the counter-example drill: a booth conversation also meets you in person. Use the structural form: every lead from this channel has already met you in person.

## The tailwind, stated with its dates

Cold email reply rates fell from 8.5% in 2019 to 3.43% in 2026 (Instantly, grade B) while AI sending tools pushed four to seven times the volume into the same inboxes (grade C plus, landing page only). Channels that can be automated are being flooded. The one that needs a person to physically turn up is not. Never extend this into "online lead generation will keep getting harder", which a published 2027 forecast contradicts.

## The wedge

The money is already being spent, and it is being spent badly. Trade show exhibiting is 40.8% of exhibitor marketing budgets (CEIR 2026, grade B). This is not a new budget request. It is the same budget, redirected to produce people who matter.

## Four statements, four comparison sets

1. **Against executive event agencies.** For an enterprise vendor whose buyers will not answer a cold approach, The Warm Channel is a lead generation channel run end to end, unlike executive event agencies, which are paid to produce an event and hand back a list of who came, because the channel's output is a sales-qualified lead, produced after the event by work the category is documented as skipping. Proof line, grade C opinion: practitioner reporting says vendors who generate pipeline from events run targeted programmes before, during and after.
2. **Against outbound and demand generation agencies.** For a marketing leader watching reply rates fall quarter on quarter, The Warm Channel is the one acquisition channel whose cost the buyer cannot drive to zero, unlike outbound agencies, paid media and SDR outsourcing, which compete on volume in channels automation is degrading, because an email costs nothing to ignore and an evening does not.
3. **Against doing it in-house.** For a team that already exhibits and already knows the return is poor, The Warm Channel is a channel operator who owns the outcome, unlike hiring a field marketing manager, which buys capacity and puts the hardest step on someone with eleven other responsibilities, because the work is six weeks of individual outreach and several weeks of follow-through, and nobody in-house has that window free.
4. **Against the status quo.** For a vendor spending the largest line in its marketing budget on events and unable to say what it produced, The Warm Channel is the same spend, redirected to produce people who matter and reported in a form a finance function accepts, unlike continuing to buy floor space and count badge scans, because the problem is not that events do not work, it is that nobody is running them as a channel. Note: "reported in a form finance accepts" describes measurement, which is IN BUILD.

| Who is in the room | Statement |
|---|---|
| A CMO deciding where next year's budget goes | 4. A reallocation argument, not a new spend |
| A demand generation lead with a falling reply rate | 2. The pain is current and theirs |
| A field marketing manager after a disappointing flagship | 1. They know what an agency handed back |
| Finance asking why the event line survives | 4, then 1 |
| A founder or CRO who does not believe in events | None. Characteristic 4 fails. Disqualify |

Statement 4 opens budget. Statement 2 is the one people repeat. Statement 1 wins a competitive evaluation once the category decision is made.

## The claim none of these makes

None says The Warm Channel produces more leads, better leads, or leads at any rate. Each is about where the work happens and who owns the outcome. That restraint is load-bearing: the argument that the category skips a step is checkable without Warm Channel data. The first delivered engagement converts these arguments into claims, and not before.

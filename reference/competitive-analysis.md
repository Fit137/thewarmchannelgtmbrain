## The axis the category runs on

**Access to an audience the operator owns, or composition of the client's own named list.** Hosted-buyer operators sell the first. The better agencies sell the second, ConvergeX described as "mapping target accounts and personally recruiting a table of genuinely senior buyers". The Warm Channel is the second, by design. Not differentiated from slot 1 on the axis, strongly differentiated from slot 4, which is the set a prospect names out loud.

## Two findings that change the selling, both grade C plus

1. **Paid-on-attendance is not unique.** Ortus is described by clients as charging only for attendees who show up. MyOutreach says no-shows are never charged. A category norm among the better operators.
2. **Competitors guarantee more.** ConvergeX is reported to guarantee at least 50% of attendees book a post-event meeting, claiming an average near 70%. CXO & Co is described as offering guaranteed attendance plus post-event meeting support. The buyer is not imagining a pipeline promise. They have been quoted one.

Confirm both on the operators' own pages before saying either on a call.

## Defensibility

Thin in year one, and it rests on guests who return. The method is copyable in a weekend. A cohort of senior people who accepted, came and came back is not. That is why The Warm Channel retains the guest list, and why a client that contracts to own it is buying the business, not the service.

## Hypotheses, never claims

No slot 1 operator surfaced selling a coverage read standalone, publishing a composition report, or focused on our segment. Absence in a search pass is weak evidence. Never write "only we" from it.

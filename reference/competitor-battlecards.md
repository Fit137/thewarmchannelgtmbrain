# Competitor battlecards

Load for: sales calls, competitive objections and comparison copy. Synthesized from `reference/competitive-analysis.md`. Each card: their real strength first, their real gap, how we win, what not to say. Weaknesses are structural unless stated, because no buyer complaint data exists.

## ConvergeX Connections, executive event agency, London-centred

- **Strength.** Named-account composition, personally recruited senior tables, and a quantified outcome: reported guarantee that at least 50% of attendees book a post-event meeting (grade C plus). Wins every bake-off decided on reportability.
- **Gap.** Sells the meeting, priced into the event. Production is in the offer, so the client hands over the evening.
- **How we win.** Select the buyer who has been burned by a promised outcome, or who wants to keep the host position and the relationships. Channel strategy across formats, not one event.
- **Do not say.** That their guarantee is false or unkept. We have no evidence. Never claim we produce more meetings.

## The Ortus Club, executive roundtables, global, Singapore origin

- **Strength.** Craft. Moderated, pitch-free rooms that executives actually attend. Praised for attendee quality, organisation and paying only for attendees who show up (review themes, C plus). Treats the guest as the scarce asset, which is the correct read of the category.
- **Gap.** Sells its own moderated format, one event at a time. Rate not public, a directory lists a minimum project size of $1,000 plus.
- **How we win.** The channel, not the room: strategy across the client's existing event calendar, and the work after the date.
- **Do not say.** Anything about their quality. Do not use pay-on-attendance against them.

## MyOutreach, delegate acquisition and invite-only dinners, UK-centred

- **Strength.** 10 to 15 C-suite per room, delegates confirmed before billing, no-shows never charged.
- **Gap.** Search text only, site not read. Treat as `{TBD}`.
- **How we win.** Same as Ortus: the channel and the follow-through.
- **Do not say.** Anything specific until the site is read.

## Clutch (clutchevents.co), B2B event marketing for technology vendors, ANZ, APAC, North America

- **Strength.** Published the benchmark the whole category quotes: 13,155 registrations across 191 events. Credible and data-literate.
- **Gap.** `{TBD}`.
- **How we win.** Cite their benchmark accurately and credit them. It makes us look precise, not smaller.
- **Do not say.** "Clutch" without "Events". Clutch.co is a review site.

## Hosted-buyer and summit operators, Evanta, Argyle, GDS Group, Millennium Alliance

- **Strength.** Scale and certainty. A real, qualified audience. Delegates contractually bound to a meeting count with a penalty for leaving early (GDS published terms, grade A).
- **Gap.** The audience is theirs. The sponsor does not choose who is in the room.
- **How we win.** "Your named accounts, not a rented audience." Every name starts on your list.
- **Do not say.** That their audiences are poor. Say whose audience it is.

## The in-house field marketing function, on software it already licenses

- **Strength.** Free at the point of decision. No procurement, no new vendor, no new line item. The competitor that wins by default while the buyer decides.
- **Gap.** No reach into executives the team cannot already contact. Software does not produce attendance. The six-week outreach window collides with everything else.
- **How we win.** Capacity, never competence. The arithmetic was never done.
- **Do not say.** Anything that sounds like the team failed. The User can kill the deal silently.

## The field marketing hire

- **Strength.** The only option that leaves an asset. A CFO understands it without explanation.
- **Gap.** Roughly $94,728 base in Toronto (ERI, B), $86,500 to $111,000 placement range in Canada (Robert Half, B plus), before a single event happens. Employer cost above base and time to first event are `{TBD}`. Split across eleven other jobs.
- **How we win.** The hire buys capacity. We run a channel this quarter.
- **Do not say.** An employer cost or time-to-hire figure. Neither is cleared.

## Trade shows and conferences, the status quo

- **Strength.** The budget already exists. Nobody is fired for exhibiting. It renews itself.
- **Gap.** $112 to $300 per badge scan, near $142 (CEIR, B). 40.8% of exhibitor budgets (CEIR 2026, B).
- **How we win.** Statement 4. Same budget, redirected. Or a programme built around a show they already exhibit at, which does not ask them to stop.
- **Do not say.** "Trade shows do not work." Say they are not run as a channel.

## Outbound, demand generation and SDR agencies

- **Strength.** Fast to start, measurable activity, cheap per contact.
- **Gap.** Reply rates 8.5% in 2019 to 3.43% in 2026 (Instantly, B).
- **How we win.** "An email costs nothing to ignore and an evening does not."
- **Do not say.** "Cold email stopped working." It returns less each year.

## Doing nothing

The named accounts stay unreached for another year. Ask what that costs. Their number, not ours.

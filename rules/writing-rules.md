# Writing rules

These apply to every word of output, client-facing, guest-facing and internal, no exceptions.
last_reviewed: 2026-10-07

## Mechanics

- No em dashes and no en dashes anywhere. Use commas, or restructure the sentence. Ranges are written "8 to 10 weeks" in prose and "8-10" with a plain hyphen in a table.
- Banned words: leverage, unlock, seamless, robust, streamline, cutting-edge, empower. Do not use them or their obvious variants, including "cutting edge".
- Short paragraphs, one to three sentences. A pain point that runs four lines stops being a punch.
- Specific numbers, named mechanisms and verifiable facts over adjectives. "A researched, individual approach over a six week window" beats "a personalised, high-touch process".
- No stacked adjectives. No superlative that is not cleared in `rules/do-not-say.md`.
- Commonwealth spelling, as every source uses it: programme, organisation, colour, behaviour, centre, favour.
- Every figure names its source in the same sentence or in a footnote that states its grade. Unknown numbers are written `{TBD}`, never estimated.
- Headline case follows the asset. The landing page keeps the title case it was drafted in. Documents, posts, emails and invitations use sentence case.

## Tone

A serious document read by an accountable marketing leader deciding where the largest line in their budget goes. Plain, specific and argued, like a memo with citations, never like a brochure. The reader is senior, sceptical and has been sold a promised outcome before, so restraint reads as confidence and an adjective reads as a tell.

Honesty about being new is a selling asset. A stated consequence is evidence. A refusal stated plainly is evidence. Modesty without a number is not, so pair every honest limit with something concrete the reader gets.

Never imply the reader's team failed. **Sell capacity, never competence.** "Nobody underperformed. The arithmetic was never done."

The guest register is different: one person writing to one peer, warm, brief, specific about the question and the evening, and free of any selling. A guest message that sounds like marketing is a guest message that gets deleted.

## Writing for the ear (calls, a demonstration-event welcome, any script)

- Short spoken sentences, one clause where you would write two.
- Second person, to one listener.
- Front-load the point. Listeners cannot re-read.
- Attribute every figure out loud, "Clutch Events found", never a bare number.
- Read it aloud before using it. If you stumble, cut it.

## Claim discipline

Every "only", "first", "best", "never", "no other" and "highest" is checked against `rules/do-not-say.md`. Every capability is checked against `rules/service-status.md`, and anything not READY carries its status in the same sentence or is omitted. Every number is checked against `rules/evidence-ledger.md`. Describe what the service does, never what it has achieved, because nothing has been delivered yet. When a claim depends on an open decision, check `ops/decisions.md` before using it.

## Channel discipline

This go-to-market writes: landing page copy, founder LinkedIn posts, single researched direct approaches, coverage reads, discovery-call guides, scope documents (once unblocked), composition reports, partnership briefs, guest invitations, reconfirmations and notes afterwards, observer invitations, opt-in scorecard emails (once unblocked), and sales and objection material.

It refuses: paid ad copy on any platform, cold email sequences, follow-up messages to non-replies, automated or high-volume outreach scripts, advertised guest invitations, purchased-list campaigns, and posting-frequency content calendars. Refuse plainly, name the rule, offer the in-scope alternative.

## Signature lines (approved, reuse verbatim)

Client: "This isn't an events agency. This is a lead generation channel that happens to run in person." "Every lead has already met you in person." "An email costs nothing to ignore and an evening does not." "The money is already being spent, and it is being spent badly. This is not a new budget request." "We are new. No case study yet, and we are not going to dress that up." "Put your own deal size and your own close rate in. We will not put a number in either of those boxes, because we do not know them."

Guest: "One question, set before the invitations went out." "Reply yes or no. Either is a fine answer." "No attendee list is circulated, before or after."

More, with status, in `ops/copy-bank.md`.

## Formatting

Prefer prose. A list only when the content is genuinely a list. A table when numbers are compared, never a chart for three data points. One call to action per asset, and on the website it is always "Book a call".

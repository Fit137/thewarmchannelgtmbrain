# The suggestion menu

Load for: choosing the brief's one suggestion.

The brief makes one suggestion. Not three options, not a menu: one idea, chosen from what the week actually shows, with its arithmetic, its honest timing, and its status where our work is mentioned. A brief that offers a menu hands the reader homework. A brief that offers one idea, well argued, gets a reply.

## Choose by what the week shows

| What the week shows | The suggestion | Format and cleared rate |
|---|---|---|
| The evening before the show opens is open, and their buyers travel in for it | A private table on the evening before the show | Invite-only executive dinner, 70% |
| They sponsor a breakfast or a reception whose seats they do not choose | Keep it, and add a small table of their own the next morning, with chosen guests | Invite-only executive dinner, 70%, as the nearest cleared rate. Say so |
| A mid-show evening is open while competitors hold the others | A private table on that evening, on a question no vendor dinner that week is asking | Invite-only executive dinner, 70% |
| Every evening is taken | No table this year. Use their own sponsored moment for this year's conversations, and put the eve-of-show table on next year's calendar now | None. No arithmetic block, say so |
| Their buyers are senior and the city has a hospitality event that week | A small group at it, for an existing relationship rather than a first conversation | VIP or sport hospitality, 86% |
| They have no in-person presence beyond a booth | Keep the booth. A table that week for the Tier 1 committees already in town | Invite-only executive dinner, 70% |

Never suggested in a brief: a bigger booth, a large reception, a sponsored panel, anything that needs the host to present, or anything that needs us to name who would attend.

## The arithmetic

The page shows the seats and the confirmations they need: people at the table ÷ the format's cleared attendance rate, rounded to the nearest whole person. Twelve seats at 70% is about seventeen confirmations. The rate and its source sit under the figure. Nothing else is estimated: how many people would need to be approached depends on their list, and stays unsaid or `{TBD}`.

## The timing, said plainly

A full table is filled by individual approaches over about six weeks. Say what the weeks left allow:

| Weeks to the show | Say |
|---|---|
| 8 or more | There is time for a full table, if the decision is made in the next fortnight |
| 6 to 8 | A full table is tight. The decision belongs in the next week or two, or the table belongs to next year |
| 4 to 6 | A full table is not realistic this year. A smaller table of people already in conversation with their team is, and next year's table goes on the calendar now |
| 3 to 4 | Nothing new to fill this year. Use what they already fund, and plan next year's |

Never soften it to make the suggestion look easy. The honesty is what makes the rest of the page believable.

## The status line

Wherever the suggestion mentions our work, its status travels with it, from the brain's service status:

- Planning a table with them: READY. "We can plan it with you now."
- Approaching guests on their behalf: BLOCKED until the written privacy position, D5. "Approaching guests on your behalf opens once our privacy position is written."
- After the evening: frozen until D11. If it is mentioned at all: "continues only with guests who choose it, designed with you, not offered until settled."

The suggestion always works without us: "You can run it with your own team." That sentence is what makes the brief a gift rather than a pitch.

# Writing rules

These apply to every word of output, no exceptions.

## Mechanics
Confirm or replace each of these with the project's own rules. They are a strong default, not doctrine.

- No em dashes anywhere. Use commas, or restructure the sentence.
- Banned words: leverage, unlock, seamless, robust, streamline, cutting-edge, empower. Do not use them or their obvious variants. {{ADD_BRAND_SPECIFIC_BANS}}
- Short paragraphs, one to three sentences.
- Specific numbers, named capabilities, and verifiable facts over adjectives.
- No stacked adjectives and no superlative that is not backed by `do-not-say.md`.

## Tone
{{TWO_OR_THREE_SENTENCES}}. Name the register, then the reason it fits this product and this audience. The reason matters more than the adjectives, because an agent can reason from "the audience is technical and accountable" and cannot reason from "professional yet friendly."

## Writing for the ear (scripts, video, audio)
Delete this section if the mix has no spoken content. Otherwise these rules replace the page rules for anything spoken.
- Short spoken sentences. One clause where you would normally write two.
- Contractions and second person. Talk to one listener, not an audience.
- Read every script aloud before it ships. If you stumble or run out of breath, cut it.
- Front-load the point of each sentence. Listeners cannot re-read.
- Signpost with spoken connective tissue, not with headers.
- Pace with variety: a short line after a long one, a marked pause, a question to reset attention.

## Claim discipline
Every "only," "first," "best," and "never" is checked against `do-not-say.md` before it ships. When a capability is unshipped, mark it coming soon or omit it, per `feature-status.md`. When a claim is only true in one mode, tier, or configuration, name the condition in the same sentence. {{MEDIUM_SPECIFIC_CLAIM_RULE}}

# The question

Load for: phase 6, and any invitation sample.

A senior guest says yes to a question, not to a host, a venue or a menu. The question is the product the guest receives. It is also what separates a table from the vendor dinner the guest has learned to decline. Get the question wrong and nothing else in the plan matters.

## What a live question is

1. **The guest is working on it this quarter.** Not a trend, a decision in front of them.
2. **Peers answer it differently.** If there is one right answer, there is no evening.
3. **No vendor can answer it for them.** If a demo answers it, it is a sales meeting with dessert.
4. **It can be asked in one line,** in the guest's words, without a product category in it.
5. **It is safe to discuss among strangers.** It never asks a guest to disclose their own organisation's weaknesses in a room.

## Anatomy

> How are you [doing the hard thing], now that [the change that made it hard]?

- "How are you governing machine identities, now that they outnumber your people?"
- "What did your last [incident class] teach you about [the measure that matters]?"
- "Where does [the problem] sit in [the budget it competes with] this year?"

The second clause carries the liveness. Without it the question is a topic.

## Finding candidates

Start from the buyer's year, not the host's product:

1. This year's agendas of the segment's conferences: which sessions exist, which are oversubscribed, which are new.
2. Regulatory and standards changes with a date attached.
3. The incident classes the trade press keeps reporting.
4. How practitioners describe the problem in public communities, in their own words.
5. What the buyer roles are being hired to fix, from job posts at the kind of organisation the guests come from.

Write five, keep three.

## Verifying: the three-signal test

A question is verified when three independent public signals show it is live now. Each signal is a register row.

| Signal | Counts |
|---|---|
| Sessions on it at this year's edition of a segment conference | Yes |
| A regulatory or standards change that forces it, with a date | Yes |
| A recurring incident class in this year's trade press | Yes |
| Practitioners discussing it in public, in their words | Yes |
| An analyst note naming it as a shift | Yes |
| The host's own marketing about it | No |

Two signals: a candidate, carried in the deck with "would be verified before any invitation goes out". One signal: cut it.

## Questions that fail

| Question | Why it fails |
|---|---|
| "Is your identity platform ready for AI?" | The host's category with a question mark on the end |
| "What are the top three trends in security?" | A topic, not a decision. Nobody gives up an evening for it |
| "How exposed is your organisation to [threat]?" | Asks a guest to disclose a weakness to strangers |
| "Which vendors are you evaluating?" | Asks the guest to do the host's research |
| "How do you measure the ROI of security?" | One clause. Live in every year, so in none |

## The host and the question

The host pays for the evening and never chooses the question. The host may decline a question that conflicts with its position, and that is all. The invitation says so in plain words: "They did not choose the question, and they are not presenting on it." A host that wants to choose the question wants a sponsored panel, which this is not.

## The sample invitation

Guest vocabulary only. The question first. Who else is coming, by role and organisation type, never by name. The host disclosed. The format. The ask. Under 120 words.

<!-- guest-copy:begin -->
Subject: One question, one evening

How are you governing machine identities, now that they outnumber your people?

I am putting a small number of security leaders who are working on it around one table. No vendors other than the host, and no consultants.

The host is paying for the evening. They did not choose the question, and they are not presenting on it.

No deck, no panel, no stage time. No attendee list is circulated, before or after.

Reply yes or no. Either is a fine answer.
<!-- guest-copy:end -->

Never in a guest's sample: anything frozen at D11 ("your details go to no one", "nothing follows"), anything that tells the guest what they are to the host, a list of who accepted, or a second message to anyone who did not reply.

## In the deck

The question slide shows the three candidates with a one-word label each and the line "Each question would be verified as live before any invitation goes out. The host never chooses the question." The invitation slide shows the sample on a card, with a client-facing side note: "A sample in the language your guests would read. It never describes them as a target."

# Event formats

Load for: phase 5, choosing what each moment can carry, and phase 7, the arithmetic.

A format is chosen from the answer, never before it: coverage first, then which moments the buyers already attend, then the format that gives the most time across a table with them. The Warm Channel supplies no venue, no catering, no production and no staffing on the day, by design. The prospect hosts and contracts those directly. What the format needs from us is the plan, the people and the conversation.

## The library

### Show-week private table

- **What:** a dinner for ten to fourteen named guests during the week of a show the prospect already exhibits at, away from the floor.
- **Fits:** the anchor moment. The buyers are in town for the show, so the table asks nothing of their calendar but one evening.
- **Seats and arithmetic:** twelve seats, about seventeen confirmations at the dinner benchmark.
- **Risks:** competitors hosting dinners the same week. The question, not the format, carries the difference. Check the saturation answer in phase 4.
- **Deck name:** "Show-week tables".

### Eve-of-conference table

- **What:** one table the evening before a sponsored conference opens.
- **Fits:** a sponsorship near the axis: the sponsor gets attention on stage and no conversation. The evening before, attendees have arrived and nothing is scheduled.
- **Deck name:** "[Conference] eve table".

### Metro table

- **What:** a quarterly table in a city where Tier 1 accounts cluster, with no show attached.
- **Fits:** a footprint with gaps, or Tier 1 accounts concentrated in a few metros, on public evidence. Never proposed in a city without that evidence.
- **Risk:** without a show, every guest travels or stays late for it, so the question has to be stronger.
- **Deck name:** "Metro tables".

### Expansion table

- **What:** a table at or beside the prospect's own conference, for customers with room to grow.
- **Fits:** the top left quadrant, own conferences.
- **Rule:** kept apart from prospects. A customer at a prospect table becomes an unpaid reference, which the guest did not agree to.
- **Deck name:** "An expansion table".

### Executive breakfast

- **What:** the morning version of a table, for buyers whose evenings belong to the show or to family.
- **Fits:** redirected breakfast-seat budgets, or a buyer group known to avoid vendor dinners.
- **Benchmark:** the ledger has no breakfast-specific rate. Use the dinner benchmark only with the words "for invite-only executive events", or none.

### VIP or sport hospitality

- **What:** a box, a match, a race day, with a small group.
- **Benchmark:** 86% attend (Clutch Events, B plus), the highest in the dataset.
- **Caution:** attention goes to the event, not the question. Good for an existing relationship, weak for a first conversation. Some buyers' organisations restrict hospitality: check the segment's norms before proposing it.

### Partner co-hosted table

- **What:** a table co-hosted with an association or a non-competing vendor already trusted by the buyer.
- **Caution:** the partner set is open at D15. Propose the shape, never name a partner as a route to guests.

## Formats the plan does not recommend, and why

| Format | Why not |
|---|---|
| A large reception at the show | A rented audience again. Whoever comes for the drinks |
| A sponsored panel | Stage time, not conversation. The host presents, which the guest promise forbids |
| A bigger booth | Scans are the problem being solved, not the solution |
| One event, run once, to see if it works | Refused or repriced, decided 2026-09-22. The output comes from work that runs for weeks |
| A webinar | Not in person. Kept off the map |

## On the day, for every format

One question, named in the invitation. No deck, no panel, no stage time beyond a welcome. Nobody pitches from the floor, including the host. No photograph of the table. No attendee list circulated, before or after. The host is disclosed and does not choose the question.

# The arithmetic

Load for: phase 7, the interactive arithmetic slide, and any sentence with a seat or confirmation count.

The arithmetic is the most persuasive slide in the deck because it is the least like marketing. It shows that a room fills by work, not by hope, and it shows it with a public benchmark and a division the reader can do in their head.

## The rule

> Confirmations needed = people in the room ÷ the format's attendance rate, rounded to the nearest whole person.

Rates come only from the evidence ledger's cleared rows (`event-formats.md` lists them). The result is said with "about": "twelve seats needs about seventeen confirmations". It is a planning figure, never a promise, and no floor is promised while D4 is open.

## Worked, at the three cleared format rates

| People in the room | Invite-only dinner, 70% | VIP or sport, 86% | Vendor-owned conference, 19% |
|---|---|---|---|
| 8 | 11 | 9 | 42 |
| 10 | 14 | 12 | 53 |
| 12 | 17 | 14 | 63 |
| 14 | 20 | 16 | 74 |
| 16 | 23 | 19 | 84 |

The third column is the argument against bringing the same buyers to a vendor-owned event: the format decides who turns up.

## Title mix

Directors and above attend at 62%, technical individual contributors at 56% with three times the no-show rate (Clutch Events, ledger row 13, B plus). These are rates across the whole dataset, not within dinners, so they are never divided into a seat count. Use them as direction: a table weighted to technical contributors needs more confirmations than the dinner benchmark suggests, and more reconfirmation work in the final week.

## What stays unknown

- **People approached.** How many named people must be approached to reach seventeen confirmations depends on the prospect's list, its titles and its relationships. It is `{TBD}` until the coverage read on their list. Never estimated from outside, never from a grade C acceptance rate.
- **Arrivals.** The benchmark predicts the category, not this table.

## The six weeks

| Window | Work | Counted |
|---|---|---|
| Weeks 6 to 4 | Research each named person, approach once, the question in the first line | Approaches made |
| Weeks 4 to 1 | Confirm, and tell everyone honestly who else is coming, by role | Confirmations against target |
| The week | Reconfirm close to the date, update anyone if the room changed | Arrivals against confirmations |

A thin room is disclosed to guests honestly at every stage. Most no-shows cancel ahead (grade C, internal planning only, so it shapes the reconfirmation step and is never quoted).

Six weeks is a method, not a claim about how long commitment takes: "We approach each person individually over a six week window." Approaching guests is BLOCKED on D5, so wherever it is described as our work, the status travels with it.

## Timing the plan

The strategy is most useful when the anchor moment is far enough ahead for its first table to be filled properly: at least eight weeks from the day the deck is sent, so the six-week window fits after a first call. Closer than that, anchor the calendar on the next edition or the next moment, and say why.

## The interactive slide

The deck's arithmetic slide lets the reader move the seat count from 6 to 40 and switch the format between the cleared rates. The confirmations recompute instantly, rounded, with no count-up animation. The third block is the approached count, `{TBD}`, labelled "set by the coverage read on your list". The note under it: "Attendance rates are the Clutch Events category benchmark, not our performance."

## What is never said

- "We fill twelve seats." Nothing is promised until D4.
- "Our show rate." There is none.
- Any reply rate, anywhere in a deck that uses the 70% figure.
- A confirmation count rounded up to look safer. The rule is the nearest whole person.
